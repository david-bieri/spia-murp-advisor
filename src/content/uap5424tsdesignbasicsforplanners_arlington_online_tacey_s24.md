## Course Description
This module course provides an overview of the typical design process — from concept to construction drawings — and explores the different ways practicing planners engage with design. Students examine types of drawings and frequently used design terminology, and develop their own design skills through interactive assignments that identify opportunities for design intervention while fostering constructive critique and collaboration.

## Learning Objectives
- Identify three different design scenarios (Conceptual Planning Design, Proposed Development Design Review, and Urban Design Intervention) and articulate the role a planner may play in each
- Develop a basic understanding of how to approach a design problem from an urban planning perspective, considering project constraints and opportunities
- Distinguish between design requirements, best practices, and project-specific design opportunities
- Understand how to contribute actively to a design process both as a team designer and in a reviewing or advising role
- Develop an original proposed Design Intervention as a culminating exercise

## Advising Notes
This intensive weekend module is well suited for MURP students seeking a practical introduction to urban design vocabulary, processes, and the planner's role within multidisciplinary design teams — particularly those with little or no formal design background. It fits naturally alongside or following coursework in land use, site planning, or development review, and complements studio-based or TOD-focused electives in the MURP curriculum. Because it is delivered as a compressed virtual module over a single weekend, it is accessible to students at any campus location, including working professionals in the NCR cohort. No prerequisites are stated, and the course explicitly prioritizes engagement over design proficiency, making it an approachable entry point for planners curious about design practice.
