## Course Description
This course introduces students to the principles and planning process of rural water supply and sanitation (W&S) services in developing countries, with a focus on appropriate technologies. It is structured from an interdisciplinary perspective, integrating engineering/science and policy viewpoints, and requires students to think as both engineer/scientist and planner/analyst. Topics range from global access to W&S services, technology design, local institutions and management structures, groundwater and hydrology, demand-oriented planning, service pricing, and community participation.

## Learning Objectives
- Describe the current level of access to, and quality of, water supply and sanitation services globally.
- Understand the design principles of several rural water supply and sanitation technologies and design critical components of those technologies.
- Understand the selection of appropriate W&S technologies aligned with different geographical settings (hills/mountains and flat plains) and user needs.
- Identify and analyze W&S stakeholders, community-based institutions, and management structures pertaining to water supply and sanitation services.
- Critically evaluate planning approaches or policies relating to the delivery of sustainable water and sanitation services.

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Global W&S access and human rights:** Current state of water and sanitation services worldwide; measuring access; water and sanitation as a human right; Sustainable Development Goal 6 and international principles.
- **Water source selection and hydrology:** Flat-plane and hill/mountain aquifer systems, water storage tanks, natural springs, groundwater development, and vulnerability to climate change.
- **Water supply technologies:** Wells, handpumps, hydraulic ram pumps, solar/electric pumps, gravity-fed systems, rainwater harvesting, and pipeline design.
- **Multiple-use water services (MUS):** Planning and implementation case studies (Nepal); existing MUS policies and scale-up strategies in developing countries.
- **Stakeholder analysis and community management:** WASH stakeholder identification and analysis; community-based institutions; community participation and sense of ownership.
- **Sanitation systems:** Latrines, EcoSan, wastewater reuse, solid waste management, stabilization ponds, constructed wetlands, sanitation marketing, and Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS).
- **Planning tools and decision-making frameworks:** Cost-benefit analysis of W&S services; risk assessment and climate-resilient planning; Human-Centered Design (HCD); compendium of sanitation technologies.
- **Special topics:** Emergency WASH; WASH and disability; WASH in schools; remaining water access challenges in the USA.
- **Paper Discussion Series (final two weeks):** Student-led presentations and discussion of scholarly articles on W&S governance, pricing, community management, and productive water use.

## Advising Notes
UAP 5324 is well suited to MURP students with a focus on international development, infrastructure planning, or environmental and water resources policy, as well as students seeking interdisciplinary technical grounding in engineering and planning approaches to global WASH challenges. The course carries no prerequisites, making it accessible at any stage of the MURP program, though it pairs naturally with other infrastructure or international development electives. Offered by an instructor based at the Northern Virginia (Arlington/NCR) campus, students should verify current campus and room scheduling when enrolling. A distinctive feature of this course is its integration of applied technology design (gravity-fed systems, groundwater, sanitation) with governance, community participation, and policy analysis, providing practical field-ready knowledge alongside academic frameworks.
