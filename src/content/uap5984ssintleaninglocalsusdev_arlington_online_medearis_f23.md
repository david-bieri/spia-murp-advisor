## Course Description
This course examines the current state of international engagement by U.S. local governments and explores how strategic, problem-focused, long-term international collaboration can produce meaningful outcomes related to the ecological, economic, and social dimensions of sustainable development at the local level. It analyzes how U.S. cities, counties, and towns can purposefully transfer and apply sustainability innovations from pioneering local governments in countries such as Germany, Denmark, and the Netherlands to advance domestic climate resiliency, green infrastructure, transportation, public health, racial justice, and social inclusion programs. Northern Virginia serves as a primary case study throughout the course.

## Learning Objectives
- Understand the evolving but underperforming role of U.S. local governments in global engagement and its effect on local sustainable development
- Evaluate current local-level international engagement plans and identify strategies to strengthen outcome orientation
- Analyze case studies of policy and technology transfer across ecological, economic, and social sustainability domains (e.g., stormwater management, transportation, energy, workforce training, racial justice)
- Assess the conditions under which cross-national exchanges—drawing on commercial, academic, research, and civil society partnerships—produce measurable local sustainability benefits
- Design and propose a strategic grant application that facilitates international knowledge transfer to a U.S. local government context

## Advising Notes
This course is well suited for MURP students interested in international planning, comparative urban policy, sustainability, and local government capacity-building, particularly those with interests in the Northern Virginia/DC metro region. It offers a distinctive practitioner-oriented capstone experience through a substantial grant application assignment that integrates policy analysis, international case research, and strategic planning skills directly applicable to local government or NGO careers. The course is taught by a practitioner-scholar and features multiple guest experts from relevant fields, making it especially valuable for students seeking professional networks in sustainability and international engagement. Students should note that the course meets virtually on Wednesday evenings and is offered through the Arlington/NCR campus; no prerequisites are stated, but a working knowledge of sustainable development concepts and U.S. local government structure will be beneficial.
