## Course Description
UAP 5234 examines the dynamics of urban economies through three core questions: What is an urban economy? How do we best understand and advance a flourishing economy at the urban or regional scale? And how does economy intersect with concerns of equity and society? The course provides students with background knowledge, language, and theories necessary for critical analysis and effective intervention where economic concerns intersect with public policy, planning, and governance. It is designed for students with little economics background but is also valuable for those seeking to apply economic reasoning to planning.

## Learning Objectives
- Identify and analyze the components of an urban economy, including political economy, finance, and governance structures
- Evaluate theories and models of urban growth, development, and change, including alternative development models
- Examine the intersections of economic forces with equity, housing, transportation, education, infrastructure, and social well-being
- Apply economic concepts and reasoning to planning problems at the urban and regional scale
- Develop skills in collaborative research, facilitation, and professional communication through team-based projects
- Critically assess development outcomes with attention to inequality, access, race, and spatial structure

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Weeks 1 & 3:** Introduction to urban economies — defining urban economy, equity, and society; components of urban economic analysis
- **Week 4:** History and drivers of urban development — land use, space, race, segregation, and uneven development
- **Week 5:** Urban prosperity and trade — export base theory, industry clusters, globalization, and metropolitan economic strategy
- **Week 6:** Labor markets — human capital, workforce development, skills mismatch, spatial mismatch, and immigration
- **Week 7:** Urban public sector — market failures, government intervention, privatization, Tiebout hypothesis, and intergenerational equity
- **Weeks 8–9:** Public education and physical infrastructure/transportation — equity in education, infrastructure investment, and public-private partnerships
- **Weeks 10–11:** Social infrastructure, public safety, land use, and housing — housing shortage, zoning, and neighborhood vitality
- **Weeks 12–13:** Resilience, climate change, and economic development — climate impacts, urban resilience, innovation districts, and equitable development models
- **Week 15:** Urban well-being in the 21st century — synthesis, assessment of flourishing cities, and forward-looking frameworks
- **Week 16:** Economic profile team presentations and final deliverables

## Advising Notes
UAP 5234 is a foundational course for the MURP program, suited to students across all concentrations who need grounding in urban economic theory, political economy, and the equity dimensions of planning practice; no prior economics background is required. The course emphasizes applied, discussion-based learning through team facilitation, a collaborative local economic portrait project, and individual analytical writing. It meets Monday evenings in Blacksburg and is co-taught by Lyon-Hill and Tate; students in the Arlington/NCR cohort should verify section availability with their advisor. The course pairs well with policy analysis, community development, and land use courses, and would ideally be taken early in the MURP sequence to build conceptual vocabulary for other coursework.
