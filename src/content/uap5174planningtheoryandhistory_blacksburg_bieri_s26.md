## Course Description
This course provides an overview of key planning theories, planning history, and planning processes and approaches. It equips students with conceptual tools to improve the quality of deliberation and develop a critical perspective on the challenges and issues facing contemporary urban planning. Topics span rational/comprehensive, incremental, communicative, and advocacy planning frameworks, examined through both historical and contemporary lenses.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Compare and contrast different planning philosophies and theories (e.g., rational/comprehensive, incremental, communicative, advocacy)
- Articulate and critique various planning theories
- Evaluate the applicability of different planning theories to planning practice and contemporary planning questions
- Analyze important events, themes, and debates in the history and evolution of planning and examine their influence on current planning thought and practice
- Examine the different ways ethical dilemmas can be framed and addressed in specific cases
- Communicate information effectively and efficiently via memos, oral presentations, and other formats

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Weeks 1–2 — Introduction:** Why study planning history and theory? Framing the structure and debates of planning theory; introduction to Jane Jacobs
- **Weeks 3–5 — Foundations:** Urban culture and society (Simmel, Mumford); planning history and visions; twentieth-century planning theory origins including zoning, the capitalist city, and housing justice
- **Week 6 — Goals, Justifications, and Critiques I:** Is urban planning even possible? Debate 1 on planning's legitimacy; uncertainty and arguments for/against planning
- **Weeks 7–8 — Diversity of Planning Thought and Critiques II:** Neoliberalism and planning orthodoxy; sustainability, resilience, spatial justice, and cosmopolitan urbanism
- **Week 9 — Theory and Practice:** Community development, minority planning perspectives, and the tension between action and theory
- **Weeks 10–11 — Collaborative and Participatory Planning:** Participatory governance; advocacy and pluralism (Davidoff); mediating difference (Forrester); Debate 2 on planning and democracy
- **Week 12 — Planning Ethics:** Professional ethics, metaethics, applied ethics, and normative ethics in planning practice
- **Week 13 — Multicultural Planning Histories:** Insurgent planning (Miraftab); smart cities; Debate 3 on global perspectives
- **Week 14 — Globalized Pluralism:** Seeing from the Global South (Watson, Shatkin); global cities and the permanent underclass
- **Week 15 — Synthesis:** What is the future of planning theory? Final paper review

## Advising Notes
UAP 5174 is a core planning theory and history course that introduces MURP students to the foundational intellectual traditions, debates, and ethical frameworks of the planning profession. Bieri's section is taught in Blacksburg and meets in-person twice weekly, making it most suitable for students on the main campus. The course has no formal prerequisites, but some prior exposure to urban history is helpful; it is typically taken early in the MURP program as a conceptual foundation for subsequent coursework in planning methods, policy, and practice. Distinctive features of this section include structured in-class debates, a semester-long group research project, and close reading of Jane Jacobs's *Death and Life of Great American Cities* alongside canonical planning theory texts. Students interested in planning thought, urban history, or the ethical dimensions of professional practice will find this course particularly engaging.
