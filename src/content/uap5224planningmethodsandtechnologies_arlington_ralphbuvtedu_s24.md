## Course Description

This course develops skills in statistical analysis and computer-based analysis techniques, with particular focus on applied descriptive and inferential statistics—including cross-tabulations, correlation, and regression analysis—as well as computer tools useful in urban planning and management. Students learn to use the statistical software STATA for Windows alongside lectures, weekly readings, problem sets, and closed-book examinations.

## Learning Objectives

- Demonstrate the use of applied statistics for planning and policy applications
- Equip students with skills to analyze and interpret data using STATA
- Help students read professional urban planning literature with an appreciation for both its substantive contributions and its use of statistical tools

## Course Topics and Schedule

- **Part I – Descriptive Statistics:** Introduction to statistics; measures of central tendency and dispersion; the normal curve; STATA Lab I (weeks 1–4)
- **Part II – Inferential Statistics:** Sampling and sampling distributions; estimation procedures; one-sample and two-sample hypothesis testing; ANOVA; Chi-square; Midterm Exam (weeks 5–8)
- **Part III – Measures of Association:** Bivariate association for nominal, ordinal, and ratio variables; STATA Lab II (asynchronous); research problem presentations (weeks 9–11)
- **Part IV – Multivariate Techniques:** Elaborating bivariate tables; simple and multiple regression; poster presentations; course recap; cumulative Final Exam (weeks 12–15)

## Advising Notes

This course is a required quantitative methods course in the MURP program and serves as a foundation in applied statistics and data analysis for planning practice. It is best suited for students who are ready to engage seriously with statistical theory and software; students with prior statistics coursework may petition to place out via a placement examination by contacting the instructor. The course meets once weekly in a computer lab and uses STATA, making access to the lab or a personal software license important, particularly for Arlington/NCR students. Students should complete this course early in their program, as its analytical skills underpin many other MURP methods and policy courses.
