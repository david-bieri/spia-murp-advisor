## Course Description
The design charrette is a critical collaborative tool in planning and design practice, taking many formats and involving diverse stakeholders. This 1-credit weekend module explores the charrette as a vehicle for collaborative design and planning, familiarizing students with design terminology and the skills needed to represent design and planning ideas in a virtual format. Students apply these skills, along with the soft skills needed to manage differing views and priorities, in a mock charrette for a design project.

## Learning Objectives
- Describe a design charrette and the design and engagement outcomes it may seek to achieve
- Develop skills to participate in a charrette as a collaborative design exercise in a virtual format
- Develop an understanding of how to coalesce differing views, design ideas, and priorities into actionable outcomes from a charrette
- Gain familiarity with design terminology commonly used in charrette settings
- Understand how planners can help guide and contribute to the charrette process to achieve equitable and transparent outcomes for projects and communities

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Day 1 — Introduction to Design Charrettes:** Course overview, introductions, and exploration of what a design charrette is; overview of the in-class charrette format; project introduction and charrette assignments; preparation and research time
- **Day 2 (Morning) — Mock Charrette Phase I:** Facilitator overview and welcome; first phase of the mock charrette collaborative design exercise
- **Day 2 (Afternoon) — Mock Charrette Phase II:** Continued collaborative design work building on Phase I outcomes
- **Day 3 — Mock Charrette Phase III & Wrap-Up:** Final phase of the mock charrette; report out and introduction of the final assignment
- **Overarching Theme — Equity and Transparency in Design:** Throughout all sessions, emphasis on how planners can make the design process transparent and equitable through the charrette format

## Advising Notes
This course is a compressed 1-credit weekend module well suited for MURP students interested in community engagement, collaborative design, and the practical tools used in planning and design practice. It requires no prior design software proficiency, making it accessible to students from diverse planning backgrounds, though a willingness to engage graphically and creatively is essential. The module complements coursework in urban design, community development, and public participation, and its intensive format makes it easy to integrate into a full course load. Because it is delivered entirely online via Zoom over a single weekend in March, students at any campus can participate without travel, though the course is associated with the Arlington/NCR offering under Instructor Tacey.
