## Course Description
This course examines the evolution of sustainable community planning concepts and tools developed in response to 20th-century sprawl, including New Urbanism, smart growth, and neo-traditional development. Students explore regulatory and financial techniques—such as urban growth boundaries, form-based codes, complete streets, transit-oriented development, and missing middle housing—used to control and guide urban development. The course evaluates these tools across different contexts (city centers, suburbs, small towns) at local, state, and some international levels, assessing both their successes and the challenges that have limited their widespread adoption.

## Learning Objectives
- Understand the historical development of sprawl and the sustainability movement's response to it
- Analyze land use regulation tools (zoning, form-based codes, TDR, urban growth boundaries) and their advantages and disadvantages
- Evaluate the relationship between transportation policy and urban growth management, including complete streets, parking, congestion pricing, and transit-oriented development
- Assess the fiscal and economic impacts of different development patterns, including the role of tax policy, TIF, and infrastructure subsidies
- Apply course concepts to real-world contexts through a final project and presentation

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Introduction & theoretical foundations:** Human settlement patterns, the nature of sprawl, sustainability concepts, and 21st-century dynamics
- **Controlling land use (Part I):** Urban growth boundaries, zoning reform, form-based codes, transfer of development rights (TDR), and bonus density mechanisms
- **Transportation & urban growth management (Part II):** New towns, suburban retrofit, state-level growth policies, U.S. road policy, complete streets, parking, and congestion pricing
- **Transit and transit-oriented development (TOD):** Public transportation history, policy, and the relationship between transit and land use
- **Fiscal and economic aspects of development patterns (Part III):** Quantifying the cost of sprawl, fiscal impact analysis, tax policy, TIF, and infrastructure subsidies
- **Guest speakers:** Practitioners including Ellen McCarthy, Gov. Parris Glendening, Barbara McCann, and Stephen C. Kenny
- **Emerging and cross-cutting issues:** Housing (ADUs, missing middle), climate crisis, stormwater management, and food security (time permitting)
- **Final project presentations:** Student group presentations in the last session (May 2)
