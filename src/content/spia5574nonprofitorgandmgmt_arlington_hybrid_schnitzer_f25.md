## Course Description
This graduate seminar acquaints students with key issues in nonprofit and nongovernmental organization (NGO) management, preparing them to serve in nonprofit, government, or for-profit institutions with close ties to such organizations. The course explores the role of civil society, organizational theory, management systems, and the blurring of sector boundaries in the United States and internationally. It uses a comparative approach drawing on both U.S. nonprofit management literature and international NGO management contexts, with particular attention to the dynamic and rapidly changing environment facing today's nonprofit managers.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Elaborate and analyze the role(s) of management in nonprofit and nongovernmental organizations and the implications for evolving social challenges.
- Identify and independently explore the context-specific management implications of various interrelationships between NPOs/NGOs and organizations across all sectors.
- Discern and integrate connections between management practice in the sector and larger themes of imagination, trust, change, power, and democracy.
- Formulate an individualized mental map and develop individualized learning frameworks for the internal and external administrative roles, tasks, and challenges confronting NPO/NGO managers in different contexts.
- Articulate and critically examine a personal orientation to the management of a NPO/NGO from one's own perspective and experience.

## Key Policies

**Attendance:**
Not explicitly stated as a formal attendance policy; however, students are expected to attend synchronous virtual class sessions (Mondays, 4:00–6:45 PM) and to complete asynchronous activities. Full participation—both in-class and on discussion boards—is a graded component comprising 25% of the final grade. Students are expected to read all materials in advance, as online postings prepare students for class and integrate directly into course presentations and dialogue.

**Late Work:**
If a student knows they will be late turning in an assignment, they should contact the instructors in advance to share their reasons so they can work together to establish a completion date. The instructors will not set new deadlines on a student's behalf; students are expected to propose a fresh deadline themselves. Once established, students are expected to meet it.

**AI / Generative AI Use:**
Students have access to the Rolai generative AI platform (a VT pilot program integrated with Canvas). Students are encouraged to use Rolai and other AI tools to enhance learning and to explore how AI can further nonprofit/NGO missions. All AI use must be fully disclosed and cited as any other information source, including quotation marks around verbatim passages and citation of paraphrased content. AI-generated chat threads used in assignments must be shared with instructors. Students are urged to avoid using AI to write portions of their writing assignments, and the Final Paper specifically encourages minimizing AI use for composition. Some assignments explicitly require interaction with AI tools.

**Academic Integrity:**
All assignments are considered graded work and are covered by the Virginia Tech Honor Code. All projects and assignments are to be completed individually unless otherwise specified. The Graduate Honor Code establishes a standard of academic integrity requiring all graduate students to exercise honesty and ethical behavior in all academic pursuits. The same academic integrity standards apply in virtual/online settings as in in-person settings.

## Course Topics and Schedule

- **Weeks 1–2 — Foundations: Introduction and Theory:** Animating themes; studying nonprofit organizations; historical background; NGO management debates; organizational theory and ambiguity; heutagogy and learner agency.
- **Week 3 — Foundations: Vision, Values, Mission, and Strategy:** Nonprofit mission constancy and responsiveness; strategic planning foundations; organizational life cycles.
- **Weeks 4–5 — Foundations: Theory of Change, Logic Models, and Accountability:** Strategic planning tools; logic model development; program evaluation; the many faces of nonprofit accountability; equity-focused evaluation.
- **Week 6 — Foundations: Governance and Life Cycle:** Nonprofit board composition and diversity; governance and board leadership; organizational life cycle stages; transparency.
- **Weeks 7–8 — Praxis: Program Design and Implementation:** NGO roles in development; service delivery, advocacy, and innovation; humanitarian coordination; expressive outputs and management sequences; case study: Pathway Home.
- **Weeks 9–10 — Praxis: People and Financial Management:** Human resource management; volunteer management; budgeting; fundraising and philanthropy; government funding; social enterprise.
- **Week 11 — Praxis: Outcomes and Performance Measurement:** Moving from outputs to outcomes; outcome indicators; equity and decolonizing evaluation.
- **Weeks 12–13 — Trust, Participation, and Collaboration:** Co-production; citizen participation; community representation; nonprofit collaboration and networks; negotiation.
- **Weeks 15–16 — Reflection, Integration, and Synthesis:** Personal synthesis of course themes; integrating foundational and praxis learning; final reflective analysis.
