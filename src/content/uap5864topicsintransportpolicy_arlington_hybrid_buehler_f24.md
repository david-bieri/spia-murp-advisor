## Course Description
This course provides an overview of urban transport planning and policy issues in the USA, surveying the recent history and current state of interdependent urban transport and land-use systems. It analyzes problems and benefits of the current transport system — including energy use, traffic congestion, environmental justice, safety, and public health — and examines the relative effectiveness and cost of proposed solutions. The political and institutional context of urban transport is also discussed.

## Learning Objectives
- Describe the interdependent development of transport systems and spatial development patterns in the USA
- Critically assess the connections among transport policies, planning, and land use
- Analyze problems of the present transport system
- Define the roles and interactions of local, regional, state, and federal levels of government in the provision and financing of transport infrastructure and services
- Critically evaluate policies and plans directed at improving the current transport system

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Introduction:** Recent travel trends, COVID-19 impacts on transportation, critical issues overview
- **Transport, Land Use, and Urban Form:** Historical development of automobile dependence, sprawl, and spatial evolution of the American metropolis
- **Alternatives to the Car:** Decline and renaissance of public transit; walking, pedestrian-friendly design, and the built environment; cycling infrastructure and equity
- **Shared and Emerging Mobility:** Carsharing, ridehailing (Uber/Lyft), bikesharing, e-scooters, and autonomous vehicles
- **Finance and Government's Role:** Gas tax, transportation finance geography, federal surface transportation law, and sustainable mobility frameworks
- **Congestion and Parking:** Congestion pricing case studies (Singapore, London, NYC), parking policy, and externalities
- **Transport Energy and Environment:** Climate change mitigation, emissions, energy data, and environmental impacts of modal choices
- **Transport Equity and Social Justice:** Environmental justice, racial equity in transit planning, ADA, Environmental Impact Statements, and freight equity
- **Traffic Safety:** Vision Zero concepts, complete streets, context-sensitive design, traffic calming, and disparities in enforcement
- **Final Presentations:** Student presentations of term papers (Weeks 14–15)

## Advising Notes
This course suits MURP students with a concentration or interest in transportation planning and policy, as well as students focused on urban sustainability, equity, or land use who want rigorous grounding in how transport systems shape metropolitan development. It covers a broad range of policy-relevant topics — finance, equity, safety, environment, and emerging mobility — making it a strong fit as a core or elective course in transportation specializations. No prerequisites are stated, but familiarity with urban planning concepts is helpful; the course draws heavily on comparative U.S. and international examples. Blacksburg-based students should note that the course is designed primarily for in-person participation at the Arlington/NCR VTRC campus, with Zoom access provided but not equivalent to the in-person lab experience; Week 11 is asynchronous for all students.
