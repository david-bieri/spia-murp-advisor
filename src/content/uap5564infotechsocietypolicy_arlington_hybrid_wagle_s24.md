## Course Description
This course examines the role and impacts of information and communication technologies (ICTs) on urban processes and governance, exploring both the opportunities ICTs offer for addressing urban challenges and the risks they pose for reinforcing or creating societal divides. Structured as a graduate seminar, the course emphasizes detailed discussion and critical analysis. The first half surveys frameworks for thinking about information technologies, while the second half applies those frameworks to five specific planning and policy fields.

## Learning Objectives
- Critically analyze the benefits and costs of emerging and existing information technologies in urban and governance contexts
- Develop and articulate informed personal positions on debates surrounding ICTs, smart cities, AI, and urban policy
- Engage with multiple disciplinary perspectives and conflicting viewpoints on technology's role in society
- Apply theoretical frameworks to applied planning fields including gentrification, digital labor, health governance, identity/privacy, and climate change
- Practice academic writing skills through book reviews and opinion/perspective pieces suitable for publication

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Foundations and Words of Caution:** Critical perspectives on technology, algorithmic oppression, big data, and hyperindividualism (Weeks 1–2)
- **Urban Theory and AI Urbanism:** AI in urban planning, big data, urban informatics, and the rise of AI urbanism in post-smart cities (Week 3)
- **Smart Cities:** Critical lenses on smart city frameworks, urban analytics, and algorithmic planning for sustainable development (Week 4)
- **Infrastructure and Environmental Costs of ICTs:** Physical realities of cloud computing, AI's water footprint, and decolonization of the internet (Week 5)
- **Intersectionality, Justice, and AI:** Digital urban justice, AI from the majority world, gender and AI, and platform urbanism (Week 6)
- **Human Decision-Making and Information Overload:** Emergency management in the digital age, information overload, and mobile technology's effects on social interaction — guest lecture by Dr. Shalini Misra (Week 7)
- **Applied Field: Gentrification and Digital Technologies:** Landlord tech, online reviews, race, and e-gentrification (Week 10)
- **Applied Field: Digital Labor:** Platform labor in the Global South, mutual aid, and professionalism after platforms — guest lecture by Dr. Noopur Raval (Week 11)
- **Applied Field: Electronic Health Governance:** Guest lecture by Dr. Nataliya Brantly (Week 13)
- **Applied Fields: Identity, Privacy, and Climate Change:** AI surveillance, facial recognition and identity construction, AI's role in climate solutions, and the carbon footprint of large language models (Weeks 14–15)

## Advising Notes
UAP 5564 is well suited for MURP students with interests in technology policy, smart cities, digital equity, environmental planning, or public health governance, as well as students considering research or policy careers involving emerging technologies. The course fulfills a critical analytical role in the MURP curriculum by bridging urban theory and applied planning fields through an ICT lens, making it complementary to courses in planning theory, housing, environmental policy, and governance. No prerequisites are stated, but the graduate seminar format assumes a baseline comfort with academic reading and discussion, and students should be prepared for a writing-intensive course with two major independent projects. The course is taught at the Arlington/NCR campus in a hybrid format, with select sessions held fully online, which may benefit students who travel or work remotely on certain weeks.
