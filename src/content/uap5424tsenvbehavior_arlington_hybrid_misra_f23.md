## Course Description
UAP 5424 examines how the design of cities, neighborhoods, and buildings affects human health and wellbeing, exploring topics such as crowding, noise, poverty, information and communication technologies, and environmental resource consumption. The course introduces major theories, concepts, research methods, and empirical findings in the field of environment-behavior studies. Students apply these frameworks to real-world urban planning, design, and public policy issues through readings, discussions, exercises, and a culminating research project.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Learn major theories, concepts, and principles of environment and behavior studies
- Appreciate the linkages between the social, cultural, and physical aspects of human environments
- Become acquainted with the research methods, tools, and techniques used in environment-behavior scholarship
- Understand and critically evaluate empirical research and its findings in the field
- Create a research evidence-based product to address a real environment-behavior issue in the local community

## Course Topics and Schedule

- **Introduction to Environment-Behavior Studies** — Scope of the field; why environment matters for health and behavior (Week 1)
- **Conceptualizing the Environment** — Ecological variables, behavior settings, human-environment relationships (Week 2)
- **Environmental Perception and Cognition** — Cognitive maps, image of the city, spatial thinking (Week 3)
- **Personal Space, Territoriality, and Crowding** — Density, defensible space, residential vulnerability (Week 4)
- **Place, Personality, and Behavior** — Environmental determinism, creative class, digital environments and youth behavior (Week 5)
- **Research Methods in Environment-Behavior Studies** — Observation, physical traces, behavioral mapping (Week 6)
- **Environmental Stressors** — Urban overload, noise, poverty, information overload, traffic (Week 7)
- **Environments and Health** — Environmental justice, urban sprawl, walkability, built environment and physical activity (Week 8)
- **Environmental Consumption and Conservation** — Tragedy of the commons, social norms, psychological barriers to climate action (Week 9)
- **Natural Environments and Psychological Restoration** — Attention restoration theory, urban green space, environmental justice (Week 10)
- **Urban Form and Design** — Livable streets, public space, social capital, third places (Week 11)
- **Technological Change** — Mobile devices, digital environments, absent presence, cognitive effects of technology (Week 12)
- **The Future of Cities** — Smart urbanism, big data, social media and civic engagement (Week 13)
- **Student-Led Case Study Discussions and Final Presentations** — Applied case analyses; individual project presentations (Weeks 15–16)

## Advising Notes
UAP 5424 is well suited for MURP students interested in the intersection of built environment design, public health, social behavior, and urban policy — particularly those pursuing concentrations in urban design, environmental planning, or community development. The course builds valuable theoretical and methodological literacy in environment-behavior research, complementing planning studio work and policy-focused electives. No prerequisites are listed, but students should be prepared for a reading-intensive graduate seminar requiring weekly written synthesis and active discussion participation. The course is offered at the Arlington/NCR campus in a hybrid format, making it accessible to Blacksburg-based students via Zoom, though Arlington-based students will benefit most from the in-person seminar experience and proximity to the instructor.
