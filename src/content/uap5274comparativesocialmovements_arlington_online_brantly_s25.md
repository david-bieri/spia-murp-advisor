## Course Description
This graduate seminar explores the origins, dynamics, and outcomes of social movements across diverse cultural, political, and historical contexts. Using a comparative perspective, students critically examine the frameworks that social movements use to mobilize, sustain themselves, and achieve their goals. Through readings, discussions, and research projects, students engage with key theories in social movement studies and develop analytical tools to evaluate why some movements succeed while others falter.

## Learning Objectives
- Understand and critically evaluate the foundations and theories of social movements
- Research and evaluate comparative frameworks examining the influence of cultural, political, and historical contexts on the strategies, behaviors, and outcomes of social movements
- Integrate varying theoretical perspectives in the analysis of social movements
- Develop critical writing and discussion skills through seminar participation and research assignments

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Introduction to Social Movements:** Definitions, scope, and historical invention of the social movement (Weeks 1–2)
- **Theorizing Social Movements:** World-systems perspectives, democratization, and futures of movements (Week 3)
- **Resource Mobilization and Political Opportunity:** Analytical frameworks, immigrant rights, Occupy movement (Week 4)
- **Civil Rights and Liberation Movements:** Black Lives Matter, mass incarceration, the Black Church, and nonviolence (Week 5)
- **Transnational and Global Movements:** Global civil society, NGOs, anti-apartheid activism (Week 6)
- **Labor Movements and Economic Justice:** Occupy Wall Street policing, union revitalization, local economic democracy (Week 9)
- **Environmental Movements:** Fracking disputes, Extinction Rebellion, climate communication strategies (Week 10)
- **Sector-Specific Movements:** Food sovereignty and struggles over global capitalism (Week 11); health social movements and medical modernization (Week 12)
- **Technology and Social Movements:** Digital organizing, online movements, open-source activism (Week 13)
- **The Future of Social Movements:** Democratic organization, synthesis, and relevance to governance challenges (Week 14)

## Advising Notes
This course suits MURP students interested in the intersections of civic engagement, policy change, and political mobilization, particularly those focused on community development, environmental planning, housing equity, or social justice. It offers a strong theoretical foundation in social movement studies that complements planning practice courses requiring an understanding of advocacy, community organizing, and stakeholder dynamics. The course is offered online-synchronously via Zoom and is cross-listed as GIA/UAP 5274, making it accessible to students across campuses; MURP students should confirm credit applicability toward their plan of study with their advisor. The comparative and research-intensive format — including a substantial final paper and case study presentation — makes this course particularly well-suited for students considering thesis tracks or policy-oriented capstone projects.
