## Course Description
A core course in the MURP program, UAP 5084 focuses on the vital roles planners and policymakers play in facilitating public engagement to enhance outcomes, build support for implementation, respond to public needs, and advance equity and inclusion. The course examines both the planner/policymaker perspective and the role of community organizers and activists in fostering grassroots participation. It combines theory, practical exercises, and direct exposure to engagement in action through project partnerships.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Critique models of deliberative democracy, framing their key features relative to stakeholder engagement and the goal of collaborative rationality
- Analyze how various tools and approaches to collaborative planning, community involvement, and alternative dispute resolution can be implemented, including associated challenges and opportunities
- Appraise overarching challenges that collaborative planning processes must address and critique how they can be ameliorated
- Analyze the roles various stakeholders can and should play in deliberating, planning, and decision-making, and what rights they have to information
- Display skills relevant to the practice of facilitation and mediation
- Select appropriate tools and approaches for collaborative planning and community involvement across different planning and policymaking contexts

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Weeks 1–3 — Foundations of Community Involvement & Deliberative Democracy:** History and varieties of public participation; Arnstein's Ladder; deliberative democracy and collaborative rationality (Dryzek, Innes & Booher); governing the commons (Ostrom, Axelrod)
- **Week 4 — Ethical and Legal Foundations:** Planning ethics (AICP Code), IAP2 Code of Ethics, FACA, FOIA, Virginia open government laws, comparative law and public participation
- **Week 5 — Infrastructure for Public Participation:** Participation in land use planning and state/federal government; designing participation infrastructure; introduction to the final group project
- **Week 6 — Thin Engagement:** Information dissemination tools and techniques; collecting public feedback; community surveys; social marketing and behavior change
- **Week 7 — Thick Engagement: Consensus Building Approach:** Consensus-building theory and practice (Susskind); managing meetings; mediating public disputes; in-class role-play simulation (*Homelessness in Niceville*)
- **Week 8 — Thick Engagement: Additional Tools and Approaches:** Student lightning talk presentations; survey of approaches including citizens' assemblies, deliberative polling, participatory budgeting, design charrettes, joint fact-finding, legislative theater, truth and reconciliation, and collaborative adaptive management
- **Week 9 — Situation and Stakeholder Assessment:** Conflict assessment, situation assessment, and stakeholder mapping as precursors to thick process design
- **Week 10 — Facilitation and Mediation:** Facilitation skills and toolkits; mediation in public disputes; planner's role in meeting facilitation
- **Week 11 — Negotiation:** Mutual gains approach; integrative negotiation; BATNA; anchoring; 3-D negotiation; application to planning and policy contexts
- **Week 12 — Embracing Diversity and Overcoming Barriers / Final Project Presentations:** Planning with diverse communities; accessibility in civic engagement; role-play debrief; final group project presentations (December 5)

## Advising Notes
UAP 5084 is a required core course for MURP students and is well-suited to students interested in public engagement practice, collaborative governance, conflict resolution, and equity in planning processes. The Blacksburg section (Schenk) emphasizes hands-on, interactive learning through role-play simulations, group projects with external partners, and weekly reflective discussions, making it particularly valuable for students who learn by doing. The course covers foundational theory (deliberative democracy, collaborative rationality) alongside practical skill-building in facilitation, mediation, and negotiation, positioning it as a bridge between planning theory and professional practice. Students should note that a parallel section is offered at Arlington/NCR (Misra); students should enroll in the section corresponding to their campus and confirm any credit or sequencing requirements with their advisor.
