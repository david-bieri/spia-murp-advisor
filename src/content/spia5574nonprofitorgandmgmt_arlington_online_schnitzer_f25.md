## Course Description
This graduate seminar acquaints students with key issues in nonprofit and nongovernmental organization (NGO) management, preparing them to serve in or alongside such organizations. Drawing on both U.S. nonprofit literature and international NGO management scholarship, the course examines civil society's role, organizational theory, management systems and functions, and the blurring of sectoral boundaries in today's political economy. The course is organized in three phases: foundational theory, applied management praxis, and reflective synthesis.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Elaborate and analyze the management roles of nonprofit and nongovernmental organizations and their implications for evolving social challenges.
- Identify and independently explore context-specific management implications of interrelationships between NPOs/NGOs and organizations across all sectors.
- Discern and integrate connections between management practice and larger themes of imagination, trust, change, power, and democracy.
- Formulate an individualized mental map and learning frameworks for internal and external administrative roles, tasks, and challenges confronting NPO/NGO managers in different contexts.
- Articulate and critically examine one's own personal orientation to NPO/NGO management from one's own perspective and experience.

Not explicitly stated as a separate attendance policy. ## Course Topics and Schedule

- **Weeks 1–2 | Foundations – Introduction and Theory:** Role of civil society, historical background of nonprofit and NGO sectors, theories of nonprofit organization, the NGO management debate, organizational theory and ambiguity.
- **Week 3 | Foundations – Vision, Values, Mission, and Strategy:** Organizational values and mission constancy, strategic direction, organizational identity and responsiveness.
- **Weeks 4–5 | Foundations – Theory of Change, Logic Models, and Accountability:** Strategic planning, logic model development, program evaluation, the many faces of nonprofit accountability, equity-focused evaluation.
- **Week 6 | Foundations – Governance and Organizational Life Cycle:** Nonprofit board composition and leadership, diversity and inclusion on boards, nonprofit life cycle stages, transparency.
- **Weeks 7–8 | Praxis – Program Design and Implementation:** NGO roles in development, service delivery, advocacy, innovation, humanitarian coordination, expressive management functions; case study: Pathway Home.
- **Weeks 9–10 | Praxis – People and Financial Management:** Human resource management, volunteer management, nonprofit financing, budgeting, fundraising, government funding, social enterprise, corporate and individual philanthropy.
- **Week 11 | Praxis – Outcomes and Performance Measurement:** Outputs vs. outcomes, evolution of performance measurement in human services, decolonizing evaluation.
- **Weeks 12–13 | Trust, Participation, and Collaboration:** Co-production, citizen participation (Arnstein's Ladder), nonprofit community-based representation, collaboration and networks, negotiation; case study: Virginia Management Fellows Program.
- **Weeks 15–16 | Reflection, Integration, and Synthesis:** Personal synthesis of course themes, integration of management frameworks, reflective analysis of management identity and practice.
