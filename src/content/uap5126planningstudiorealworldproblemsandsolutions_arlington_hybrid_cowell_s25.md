## Course Description
This studio course consists of individual and collaborative group work on a community client-based project focused on childcare accessibility in Northern Virginia. Students work with real clients — the Arlington Community Foundation (ACF), the Northern Virginia Transportation Commission (NVTC), and Virginia Tech's Center for Economic and Community Engagement (CECE) — on projects connecting childcare policy to economic development, transit, and planning. Students gain skills in project management, problem identification, data collection and analysis, community/stakeholder engagement, and professional norms and practices.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Assess the strengths and weaknesses of different types of data
- Design and implement data/information collection programs
- Analyze data and policy-relevant information
- Effectively communicate research findings orally and in writing
- Apply professional norms and practices in a client-facing planning context
- Develop skills in project management and stakeholder engagement

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Weeks 1–2 (late January – early February):** Introductions, best practices, getting organized; meet with clients; form teams; discuss content and scope of work; review existing studies; prepare IRB if needed
- **Weeks 3–6 (February – early March):** Team-based group work; IRB approval process; initial data collection and scoping; begin outlining final report and identifying graphics
- **Spring Break (March 10):** No class
- **Weeks 7–10 (mid-March – early April):** Continued group work; writing methods sections; creating graphics; GIS, field research, data cleaning, and analysis
- **Weeks 11–13 (mid–late April):** Writing final report sections; internal review; preparation of final presentation; second complete draft sent to client
- **Final week (~May 4):** Presentation to clients (date TBD)
- **Cross-cutting themes:** Transit-accessible childcare (NVTC project); zoning/permitting and financial barriers to childcare facilities (ACF project); comparative state/national childcare policy analysis (CECE project)

## Advising Notes
UAP 5126 is the Planning Studio course in the MURP program, designed for students ready to apply planning skills in a real-world, client-facing setting; it is best suited for students who have completed foundational MURP coursework in planning methods, policy analysis, and GIS. This section is taught at the Arlington/NCR (Ballston) campus and focuses on childcare accessibility in Northern Virginia, connecting planning to transit, economic development, and zoning policy — topics highly relevant to students with interests in housing, transportation, community development, or social equity planning. The studio structure means there is no traditional reading list; instead, students must be self-directed, collaborative, and comfortable managing deliverables on a team timeline with external client accountability. Students in the Blacksburg or National Capital Region cohorts considering cross-campus enrollment should confirm scheduling compatibility, as this section meets Mondays 7:00–9:45pm and is primarily in-person in Ballston.
