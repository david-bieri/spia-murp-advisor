## Course Description
This course provides a graduate-level introduction to concepts central to effective financial management in governmental and nonprofit organizations. It reviews the components of financial management and foundational concepts in budgeting, accounting, financial statements and management reporting, auditing, internal control systems, and accountability. The course prepares students to analyze financial documents and assess the financial condition of public and not-for-profit entities.

## Learning Objectives
- Become familiar with the fundamental concepts of financial management in public and nonprofit organizations
- Employ financial management tools in analyzing and understanding the financial conditions of public and nonprofit organizations
- Become familiar with basic literature — both historical and current — in public and nonprofit financial management

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Overview of Financial Management:** Differences among governmental, nonprofit, and for-profit entities; organization of the financial function; roles of financial officers; components such as treasury, risk, cash management, auditing, and purchasing (Classes 1–2, Aug. 24 & 31)
- **Planning and Budgeting:** Linking comprehensive and financial planning to budgeting; performance-based budgeting; impact of budget shortfalls and managerial responses (Classes 3–4, Sept. 14 & 21)
- **Capital Budgeting and Long-Term Financing:** Capital asset planning, debt management, debt capacity, and long-term financing strategies (Classes 5–6, Sept. 28 & Oct. 5)
- **Budget Document Review and Presentation:** Application of GFOA Distinguished Budget Presentation Award criteria to a real budget document (Class 7, Oct. 12)
- **Treasury Administration, Investments, and Pensions:** Cash management, endowments, public pension funds, and OPEB (Class 8, Oct. 19)
- **Accountability and Internal Controls:** Defining and implementing systems of internal control; ethical dimensions of financial management (Class 9, Oct. 26)
- **Accounting and Auditing for Governments and Nonprofits:** GAAP, GASB, FASB, FASAB standards; fund accounting; bases of accounting; audit types and processes; CAFRs (Classes 10–11, Nov. 2 & 9)
- **Financial Statement Analysis and Presentations:** General-purpose financials; MD&A; statements of net position, activities, and cash flows; student presentations of annual financial results (Class 12, Nov. 16)
- **Financial Resiliency and Condition Analysis:** Ratio analysis; financial condition vs. financial statement analysis; synthesizing course concepts (Class 13, Nov. 30)
- **Student Research Paper Presentations:** Final papers submitted and presented (Class 14, Dec. 7)

## Advising Notes
SPIA 5024 is a foundational course in public and nonprofit financial management suited for MURP students and other SPIA graduate students who need working knowledge of budgeting, accounting, financial statements, auditing, and financial condition analysis in governmental and nonprofit contexts. It serves as a core course for the Graduate Certificate in Public and Nonprofit Financial Management and pairs well with planning finance and policy analysis coursework in the MURP curriculum. The course is taught by an instructor based in the Richmond area and delivered in a hybrid format via weekly Zoom sessions, making it accessible to students at the Arlington/NCR campus. Students should be prepared for a moderately heavy reading load, a multi-stage research paper, and practical assignments involving real budget documents and financial statements.
