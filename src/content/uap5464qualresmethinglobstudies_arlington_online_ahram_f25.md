## Course Description
This course introduces advanced master's and Ph.D. students to key topics in qualitative and multi-method research for the social sciences. Part I provides a broad overview of methodological concerns in qualitative research, including causation, hypothesis development, mechanism, and concept construction. Part II exposes students to key methodological tools used in social science research, including use of archival sources, secondary sources, and interviews.

## Learning Objectives
- Classify the major differences between qualitative and quantitative methodology
- Appraise existing research in its application of comparative methodology, particularly qualitative methodologies involving case studies
- Design research programs that involve various forms of qualitative comparison
- Defend and judge methodological choices in research design, including the drawbacks and advantages of specific research strategies

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Introduction to Research Design:** Research questions, positionality, and how scholars approach inquiry (Week 1)
- **Naming, Counting, and Epistemology:** Quantitative vs. qualitative logic; researcher reflexivity (Week 2)
- **Theory Building and Ontology:** Causation, ontological alignment, and methodological frameworks (Week 3)
- **Explanations, Evidence, and Models:** Model-making, positive economics, thick description, and causal reasoning (Week 4)
- **Case Selection:** Selection bias, small-N logic, and comparative case study strategies (Week 6)
- **Academic Writing for Social Scientists:** Argumentation, literature reviews, and scholarly prose craft (Week 7)
- **Mechanistic Explanation and Process Tracing:** Contingent generalizations, causal mechanisms, and temporality (Week 8)
- **Working with Texts and Content Analysis:** QDA software introduction; content analysis methods (Week 9)
- **Concepts and Measurement:** Conceptual stretching, measurement strategies, thick vs. thin concepts (Week 10)
- **Case Study in Concepts — Peace:** Application of concept-building methods through the literature on peace (Week 11)
- **Interviews:** Elite interviewing, capturing cause and effect, vulnerable populations, expert interviews (Week 12)
- **Archival and Secondary Sources:** Historiography, pragmatic archival methods, institutional analysis (Week 13)
- **Process Tracing (Advanced):** Practice tracing, limits of safety case, philosophical foundations (Week 15)
- **Multimethod Research:** Nested analysis, mixed-method design, methodological pluralism (Week 16)

## Advising Notes
This course is well suited to MURP students with a comparative, international, or policy-focused research agenda, particularly those pursuing a thesis or who need to develop a rigorous qualitative research design for a capstone project. It fulfills a methods requirement and pairs naturally with quantitative methods coursework, offering complementary training in case study logic, interviewing, archival research, and concept development. Students should be prepared for graduate-level reading loads and substantive writing assignments; the course's scaffolded essay sequence culminates in a full research design proposal that can serve directly as a thesis or dissertation prospectus. Delivered fully online via Zoom on Tuesday evenings, the course is accessible to Arlington/NCR-based students without requiring travel, and is cross-listed as GIA 5464, reflecting its interdisciplinary relevance across planning and international affairs programs.
