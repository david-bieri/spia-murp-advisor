## Course Description
This course provides graduate-level concepts central to effective financial management in complex governmental, nonprofit, and nongovernmental organizations. It prepares students and professionals with an understanding and working knowledge of the role of finance in organizational management, rather than a technique-oriented approach. Topics include planning and budgeting, cash and debt management, resource acquisition, accounting and financial reporting, auditing, internal controls, and financial resiliency.

## Learning Objectives
- Become familiar with the fundamental concepts of financial management in public and nonprofit organizations
- Employ financial management tools to analyze and understand the financial conditions of public and nonprofit organizations
- Become familiar with basic literature in public and nonprofit financial management, both historical and current
- Complete a critical analysis of a substantive financial management concept as applied to a public or nonprofit organization or sector
- Understand the relationship between financial accounting and reporting systems and effective organizational management

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Module I – Financial Management, Planning and Budgeting:** Introduction to public financial management; differences among governmental, nonprofit, and for-profit entities; comprehensive and financial planning; budget processes and techniques; nonprofit budgeting challenges; capital planning and budgeting
- **Module II – Implementation and Controlling Results:** Long-term financing and debt management; resource acquisition and grant management; partnerships and intergovernmental relations; treasury and investment management; endowment and pension fund management; accountability, budget monitoring, performance management, and internal controls
- **Module III – Reporting Results:** Accounting standards (GAAP, GASB, FASB, FASAB, IRS Form 990); fund accounting; bases of accounting; auditing types and processes; financial statement concepts including ACFRs, Statement of Net Position, Statement of Activities, and Cash Flow Statements
- **Module IV – Financial Condition Analysis and Resiliency:** Analysis of financial condition; financial resiliency frameworks; assessment tools for governmental and nonprofit entities
- **Module V – Finale:** Student final research paper presentations; course synthesis and wrap-up
- **Milestone-Based Research Project (running throughout semester):** Progressive assignments building toward a 10–15 page research paper on a selected public or nonprofit organization, incorporating interviews with at least two affiliated individuals, and culminating in an in-class PowerPoint presentation

## Advising Notes
SPIA 5544 is well suited for MURP students interested in the financial management dimensions of public agencies, local governments, or nonprofit organizations—particularly those pursuing planning roles that involve budgeting, grant management, or capital planning. The course is a required component of the Graduate Certificate in Public and Nonprofit Financial Management and may also count toward the Graduate Certificate in Nonprofit and Nongovernmental Management, making it a strong elective for students seeking a formal credential alongside their MURP degree. No formal prerequisites are listed, but students should be prepared for a graduate seminar format with substantial reading, writing, and research expectations; the milestone structure rewards early and sustained engagement with a chosen organization. The course is fully online-synchronous via Zoom on Wednesday evenings, making it accessible to students at any campus or in professional roles, though reliable internet and active weekly participation are essential.
