## Course Description
This course in urban transport planning focuses on concepts, methods, and applications for sustainable transport. While all modes of daily urban travel are considered, the course emphasizes policy and planning for bicycling and walking. Topics include sustainable transport in the United States, data sources for transport planning, the urban transport planning process, regional travel demand forecasting, traffic calming, and planning for bicycling and walking, with guest speakers highlighting aspects of planning for more sustainable transport.

## Learning Objectives
- Describe and critically assess the traditional 4-step regional transport planning process
- Critically evaluate the quality and assumptions of data and data sources for transport planning for sustainable transport
- Analyze user needs and demand for walking and cycling and their safety
- Define and differentiate types of infrastructure and policy measures to promote walking and cycling in cities

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Introduction to Transportation Planning:** History, decision-making, system characteristics, and the state of the U.S. transport system
- **Travel Demand Forecasting I–III:** The traditional 4-step planning process, trip generation, parking standards, and demand analysis methods
- **Regional and Metropolitan Planning:** MPO processes, federal transportation planning requirements, and the role of COGs (with guest speakers from MWCOG)
- **Data Sources for Transportation Planning:** National Household Travel Survey (NHTS), StreetLight Data, U.S. Census/ACS, and activity-based demand models
- **Planning for Bicycling — Context and Demand:** State of cycling in the USA, international comparisons, cyclist typologies, and network connectivity/low-stress bicycling
- **Planning for Bicycling — Infrastructure:** Bike facility history (AASHTO), separated infrastructure, bikeway networks, and federal perspectives (FHWA)
- **Bicycle and Pedestrian Master Plans:** Frameworks for developing, evaluating, and implementing citywide active transportation plans
- **Planning for Pedestrians and Traffic Calming:** Pedestrian safety, dangerous street design, traffic calming techniques, and the built environment
- **Emerging Modes — E-Scooters:** Benefits, barriers, safety, parking, and integration into urban transportation systems
- **Final Presentations:** Student paper presentations; final papers due before the last session

## Advising Notes
This course is well-suited for MURP students with interests in transportation planning, active transportation, sustainable mobility, or urban design, particularly those interested in the Washington, D.C./Northern Virginia metropolitan context. It fits naturally within the Transportation Planning specialization and complements courses in land use, environmental planning, and GIS. The course features a mix of quantitative methods (travel demand modeling) and qualitative policy analysis, alongside recurring guest speakers from MWCOG and federal agencies, providing strong professional networking opportunities for students at the Arlington/NCR campus. No formal prerequisites are listed, but students should be prepared for a significant reading load and an independent research paper.
