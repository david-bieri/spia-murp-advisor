# MURP Program — Curriculum, Faculty, and Program Information

Source: spia.vt.edu/academics/graduate/murp.html
Last updated: May 2026

## Program Overview

The Master of Urban and Regional Planning (MURP) at Virginia Tech is a 48-credit
professional degree offered through the Urban and Environmental Policy and Planning (UEPP)
program within the School of Public and International Affairs (SPIA).

- PAB-accredited
- Planetizen ranking: #1 in Virginia, notable small-city program nationally
- AICP exam pass rate: 100% (2021 graduates taking within 3 years)
- Employment in planning or planning-related positions within 12 months: 88% (2024 class)
- Retention rate: 96% (fall 2024 to fall 2025 cohort)
- Graduation rate within 4 years: 89% (entering class of 2021)
- Degrees awarded 2024–2025: 19

## Credit Requirements

- Total: 48 credits
- Core courses: 18 credits (6 required courses × 3 credits each)
- Electives: 24 credits (chosen with advisor)
- Capstone or thesis: 6 credits

## Core Courses (18 credits — all required)

### UAP 5014 — Gateway to Planning (3 credits)
Careers in planning across public, private, and nonprofit sectors. Planning ethics,
community engagement, social justice, professional regulations. Dimensions of complex
planning problems. Professional skill development including written, oral, visual,
and digital communication.

### UAP 5084 — Collaborative Planning and Community Involvement (3 credits)
Public participation in planning and decision-making. Deliberative democracy and citizen
empowerment. Tools for community involvement and alternative dispute resolution.
Facilitation and engagement process design.

### UAP 5174 — Planning Theory and History (3 credits)
Key planning theories and history of planning thought and practice. Critical perspectives
on contemporary planning challenges. Milestone events, themes, and debates in planning
history. Ethical issues in planning.
Taught by: Prof. David Bieri (Blacksburg) and Prof. Maggie Cowell (Arlington).

### UAP 5224 — Planning Methods and Technologies (3 credits)
Quantitative and qualitative research methods for urban and regional planning. Data types,
sources, and preparation. Survey research. Technologies for urban planning and analysis.
Ethics in planning methods.

### UAP 5234 — Urban Economy, Equity, and Society (3 credits)
Urban and regional political economy and finance. Economic forces and political
institutions and their effects on urban policy, infrastructure, and inequities.
Urban growth and change, real estate markets, urban finance, taxation policy,
and local revenue sources.

### UAP 5554 — Land Use and Planning Law (3 credits)
Fundamentals of land use law in the United States. Constitutional and statutory
limitations on regulation. Common law principles. Traditional zoning processes.
Smart growth techniques and form-based codes. Relationship between land use regulation
and equity, sustainability, and environmental justice.

## Elective Concentrations (24 credits, selected with advisor)

Students work with their advisor to design an individualized concentration.
Standard concentrations:
- Environmental Policy and Planning
- Land Use and Physical Development Planning
- Housing, Community, and Economic Development
- International Development Planning
- Transportation Planning and Policy
- Planning Analytics
- Urban Design
- Custom concentration (with advisor approval)

See murp_electives.md for specific courses supporting each concentration.

## Capstone Options (6 credits — choose one)

### Option A: Planning Studio
UAP 5125 + UAP 5126 (3 + 3 credits) — two-semester studio
- Fall: client-based problem identification, data collection, community engagement,
  project management
- Spring: data analysis and visualization, solution proposals, written report,
  oral presentation
- Practically oriented with real community clients
- Past projects: bikeshare feasibility studies, affordable housing analysis,
  automated vehicle planning
- Best for students pursuing professional practice

### Option B: Master Research and Thesis
UAP 5994 (6 credits)
- Original research using primary or secondary data, documentary, or ethnographic sources
- Requires a committee and faculty advisor
- Students planning the thesis should discuss with a potential advisor in year one
- Best for students considering doctoral study or pursuing original research contributions

## Campus Locations and Differences

Both campuses provide access to all UEPP faculty, joint studios, virtual courses,
and the option to switch campus during the degree.

### Blacksburg
- Full-time focus; daytime classes
- More assistantship opportunities across VT
- Extensive university resources (career services, health, job fairs)
- Access to rural, small-town, and Appalachian planning contexts (Roanoke, New River Valley)
- Low cost of living
- Proximity to Blue Ridge and Allegheny mountains, outdoor recreation

### Arlington (Northern Virginia / DC Metro)
- Evening classes only: 4:00–6:45 PM and 7:00–9:45 PM
- Ideal for working professionals; part-time friendly
- Access to federal agencies, national associations, think tanks, local governments
- Strong job market in public and private sectors
- Access to DC metro resources and Capital Area Student Planning Association (CASPA)
- Largest concentration of VT alumni

## Community Scholars Fellowship

Partnership with Virginia Chapter of APA to encourage diversity in planning.
Open to admitted MURP students from underrepresented groups (African American, Latinx,
Pacific Islander, American Indian). Apply with additional essay by March 1 financial
aid deadline. Funding-dependent.
