## Course Description
This course provides an introduction to the profession of planning and the roles of planners, examining how planners in the public, private, and non-profit sectors tackle social, physical, environmental, and economic dimensions of complex problems. It focuses on planning ethics, community and stakeholder engagement, social justice, and professional regulations. It aims to provide professional skill development with a focus on written, oral, and visual communication.

## Learning Objectives
- Investigate the role and responsibilities of the professional planner and how these vary by sector (public/private/nonprofit) or by context or governance system (municipality/state/nation).
- Analyze the social, physical, environmental, economic, and other dimensions of planning challenges.
- Develop and present information in written, visual, oral, and digital forms suitable for multiple audiences (researchers, policymakers, professional staff, or the public) in factually accurate, legally consistent, and grammatically correct form.

## Advising Notes
UAP 5014 Gateway to Planning is the foundational gateway course for incoming MURP students and is designed to orient new graduate students to the planning profession, its ethics, and its practices. The Arlington/NCR section is taught by Prof. Cowell and is well-suited for students entering the program at the Arlington campus; several sessions are held jointly with the Blacksburg section (taught by Prof. Zahm), providing cross-campus cohort connections. The course emphasizes professional skill-building — written, oral, and visual communication — alongside substantive introduction to planning theory and social justice, making it an essential first-semester experience for all MURP students. Students should note that no prerequisites are listed, but the course is intended as a first-semester requirement and sets the foundation for the full plan of study.
