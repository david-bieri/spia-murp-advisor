## Course Description
UAP 5234 is a core course that introduces planning and policy students to urban and regional political economy and finance, examining how economic forces and political institutions shape urban policy, governance, physical and social infrastructure, and inequities. Topics include urban growth and change, real estate markets, local and federal governance, housing, and institutional access. The course is designed for students with little background in economics, while also deepening applied economic reasoning for those who have prior economics training.

## Learning Objectives
- Develop background knowledge, language, and theories necessary for critical analysis of urban and regional political economy
- Understand interactions between economic forces and political institutions as they affect urban policy and governance
- Analyze issues of equity, housing, labor markets, public education, infrastructure, and land use through an economic lens
- Apply economic reasoning to planning practice and effective intervention in public policy
- Engage critically with urban growth, change, economic decline, and regional governance

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **U.S. Metro Areas in the 20th and early 21st Centuries** — historical development of metropolitan areas, redlining, racial covenants, deindustrialization, and COVID-era changes (Weeks 1, 3–4)
- **Urban Prosperity and Trade** — export bases, globalization, free trade, and local economic clustering (Week 5)
- **Urban Labor Markets** — workforce development, skills, AI and automation, good jobs policy (Week 6)
- **Urban Public Education and Market Prosperity** — education reform, inequality, college attainment, and urban economic outcomes (Week 7)
- **Market Failures, Externalities, and the Public Sector** — Tiebout model, suburban sprawl, just city frameworks, and the role of government (Week 8)
- **Physical and Social Infrastructure** — public vs. private infrastructure, transit, parks, libraries, equitable health outcomes (Weeks 9–10)
- **Housing Markets** — affordability, missing middle, reparations, low-income housing access (Week 11)
- **Land Use, Zoning, Sprawl, and Smart Growth** — zoning reform, educational inequality, equitable and resilient development (Week 12)
- **Economic Development Theory and Practice** — innovation districts, community economic development, American Rescue Plan, COVID-19 effects on urban economies (Weeks 13, 15)
- **Student Presentations: Local Economic Portraits** — applied capstone presentations synthesizing course concepts (Week 16)

## Advising Notes
UAP 5234 is a required core course in the MURP program, making it essential for all incoming students regardless of specialization; it is particularly well-suited to students without prior economics training who need a strong foundation in urban political economy, housing, labor markets, and public finance. The course integrates equity and social justice frameworks throughout, making it relevant for students interested in community development, housing policy, economic development, and planning practice. This section is taught at the Arlington/NCR campus on Monday evenings in a hybrid format, which suits working professionals in the DC metro area; Blacksburg-based students should enroll in sections taught by Lyon-Hill or Tate. No formal prerequisites are listed, and the course is explicitly designed to be accessible to students with little or no economics background.
