## Course Description
This course addresses land use analysis and management to protect environmental resources and improve land development, with a focus on the United States. Drawing from planning, geography, design, land use law, public policy, economics, natural science, and engineering, the course emphasizes analytical planning techniques through problem assignments. Graduate students enrolled for UAP 5134G credit complete an additional short term paper or project (maximum 8 pages) exploring a specific environmental land use problem, analytical or policy approach, or innovative case study.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Develop knowledge of the relationship between land use and the natural environment
- Apply methods and techniques of environmental land analysis
- Assess technologies for mitigation of environmental impacts
- Identify principles of environmentally sensitive land use, design, and development
- Assess and apply policies and programs to control land use impacts and to protect and restore environmental resources

## Course Topics and Schedule

- **Part I – Introduction to Environmental Land Use Planning:** Overview of environmental management and planning frameworks; collaborative planning approaches; geospatial information and mapping (Weeks 1–2)
- **Part II – Soils, Water, and Land Use:** Soils analysis, suitability mapping, and soil erosion; water quality and peak discharge; stormwater management and low-impact development (LID); stream restoration (Weeks 3–5)
- **Groundwater and Ecology:** Groundwater protection (DRASTIC, wellhead protection); urban ecology; wetlands; ecological analysis (Week 6)
- **Wildlife, Biodiversity, and Natural Hazards:** Habitat conservation; ecosystem management; natural hazards mitigation (Week 7)
- **Land Conservation and Integrative Methods:** Conservation easements; design with nature; environmental impact assessment (EIA); land suitability analysis; inventories (Weeks 9–10)
- **Part III – Energy, Air Quality, and Land Use:** Climate change mitigation; land use–transportation–air quality linkages; climate action plans; adaptation strategies (Weeks 11–12)
- **Smart Growth and Energy-Efficient Urban Form:** Walkability; smart growth principles; energy-efficient design (Week 13)
- **Governance and Policy:** U.S. local, state, and regional government frameworks for land use and environmental planning (Week 15)
- **Assessments:** Exam #1 (Week 8); Exam #2 and graduate term papers due (Week 16)

## Advising Notes
UAP 5134G is well-suited for MURP students with interests in environmental planning, land use regulation, sustainability, or natural resource management. The course provides a strong interdisciplinary foundation spanning planning law, policy, ecology, and geospatial analysis, and is particularly valuable for students pursuing environmental or land use planning concentrations. The graduate section requires an additional term paper (max 8 pages) on a specific environmental land use topic, giving students the opportunity to develop specialized knowledge or a portfolio piece. Offered fully online and asynchronously, this course is accessible to both Blacksburg and Arlington/NCR students, though the instructor is based at the Blacksburg campus (Architecture Annex); students should plan to engage primarily via Canvas and schedule appointments as needed.
