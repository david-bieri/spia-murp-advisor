## Course Description
This 2-day module examines what makes for successful transit-oriented development (TOD), the obstacles to its implementation, and errors to avoid. Drawing on Arlington's Rosslyn-Ballston corridor as one of North America's premier TOD examples, the course explores both theoretical considerations and practical implementation issues for policymakers and developers. It also addresses challenges accompanying TOD success, particularly social equity and affordable housing.

## Learning Objectives
- Be familiar with the policy issues involved in TOD, including land use planning, urban design, transit operations, regional issues, finance, place management, and social equity.
- Understand the benefits that can be achieved with TOD and the challenges to realizing them.
- Recognize the difference between development that is transit-oriented and that which is merely transit-adjacent.
- Be able to analyze development proposals and identify salient features that make them broadly consistent (or not) with TOD objectives.
- Understand the roles of the private and public sectors in creating transit-oriented development.
- Understand how Arlington, Virginia used TOD as a strategy to transform a declining inner-ring suburb into a dynamic, fiscally and environmentally sustainable center of activity.

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **TOD overview and rationale:** Why TOD matters now; suburban transformation imperative; real estate values; climate change; equity and infrastructure challenges
- **Foundations of TOD:** Definitions, urban and transportation history, factors driving demand, demographic and lifestyle trends
- **Transit and urban form:** Relationship between transit and development patterns; transit operations and economics; modes; network design
- **Urban design at the station level:** The "Ds" framework; TOD vs. transit-adjacent development (TAD); geometry of TOD (¼ and ½ mile radii); density, use mix, parking, and pedestrian environment
- **Implementation challenges:** Land use regulation, Virginia planning law, zoning and proffers, financing, value capture (TIF, special assessments, joint development), and institutional barriers
- **Social equity and affordable housing:** Gentrification concerns; equitable TOD (eTOD); legacy business issues; housing affordability strategies
- **Major case study — Arlington's Rosslyn-Ballston Corridor:** History, policies, and outcomes; National Landing, East Falls Church, Shirlington Village, Columbia Pike
- **Field study:** Walking seminar through station sectors of the R-B Corridor; on-the-ground observation of density gradients, use mix, urban design, and transitions
- **Regional TOD:** Benefits of regional planning for TOD; urban spatial structure; replicability of the Arlington model
- **Supplementary topics (time permitting):** Transportation Demand Management (TDM), car- and bike-sharing, place management, public engagement

## Advising Notes
This course is well-suited for MURP students with interests in transportation planning, land use, urban design, or sustainable development, particularly those focused on metropolitan and suburban contexts. As a 1-credit intensive module, it complements full-semester courses in transportation, land use law, or housing policy and can help students build a focused understanding of TOD as both a planning concept and a practical implementation strategy. The course is offered exclusively at the Arlington/NCR campus and includes a field component along the Rosslyn-Ballston Corridor, making it especially relevant for students engaged with Northern Virginia planning practice. Students should note that no prerequisites are listed, but background in urban planning fundamentals will enhance engagement with the material.
