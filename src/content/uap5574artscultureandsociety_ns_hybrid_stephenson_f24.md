## Course Description
Arts, Culture and Society is an interdisciplinary seminar designed to establish philosophical bases, vocabularies, and hands-on approaches for leadership in community-based, grassroots theatre ensembles and nonprofit/nongovernmental organizations engaged in social change. The seminar builds a foundation for personal goal setting and professional planning while developing students' capacity for intentional self-reflection. It considers particularly the dynamics of arts and cultural initiatives aimed at securing cross-sector cultural change for social and economic justice.

## Learning Objectives
- Describe the evolving field of Community Cultural Development as both a practice and a domain of scholarly research
- Chart the historical evolution of community cultural development efforts from the late 19th century to the present
- Relate theories of the field to its practices by researching specific arts ensembles or nonprofit organizations and comparing their efforts to relevant academic literature
- Engage in a strategic development process for personal and organizational planning, problem solving, and decision-making
- Describe how ensembles and other nonprofit arts organizations differ from and resemble one another
- Develop appropriate criteria for assessing social change-oriented project success
- Outline the functions, responsibilities, and central ethical challenges of the arts/community development professional
- Identify communities of specific interest and outline ways of developing relationships within those communities
- Sketch the central characteristics, challenges, and processes involved in securing and sustaining community-based partnerships and collaborative networks

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Community Cultural Development foundations:** History, theory, and practice of integrating arts into community life from the late 19th century to the present (August–September)
- **Emergent Strategy and personal/organizational self-assessment:** Brown's framework for adaptive, resilient, justice-oriented organizing; individual and partner reflection exercises (August–October)
- **Arts as social practice and social change:** Readings across Burrough/Walgren, Grossinger, and Goldbard connecting art-making to civic dialogue, political mobilization, and nonprofit leadership (September–October)
- **Black radical imagination and freedom dreams:** Kelley's frameworks for visionary social change; race, capitalism, and justice (September–October)
- **Social imaginaries and aesthetics:** Taylor's theory of modern social imaginaries; Marcuse on aesthetics and political change; Leonard on theatre and social imaginaries (September–October)
- **Cross-sector partnerships and nonprofit organizational structure:** Partnership frameworks, ensemble structure, NGO/CSO design, and community-based network building (October)
- **Profiling arts and nonprofit organizations:** Research and critical analysis of real-world ensembles and organizations; connecting mission, structure, and social change theories (October–November)
- **Field practitioner conversations:** Student-led interviews with practitioners; connecting theory to lived professional experience (November)
- **Personal and career planning:** Strategic planning profiles for individual professional trajectories using Planning Profile framework (November–December)
