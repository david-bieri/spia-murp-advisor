## Course Description
This course examines the fundamentals of law as applied to land use, with a primary focus on practice in the United States, particularly Virginia. It covers constitutional and statutory limitations on regulation, common law principles, traditional and alternative zoning approaches, subdivision and environmental law, and the relationship between land use regulation and broader urban issues such as social segregation, sustainability, and environmental justice. The course provides practical preparation for planners working in local government or the private sector to navigate relevant legal frameworks.

## Learning Objectives
- Develop a foundational understanding of the law applicable to the use and development of land, including planning, zoning, subdivision, and environmental laws
- Understand the significant role of the U.S. and state constitutions in shaping and limiting land use regulation
- Examine the history, intended and unintended consequences, and evolution of land use laws as they address current and future planning challenges
- Gain familiarity with both federal and state enabling laws that shape the scope and nature of local land use authority, with emphasis on Virginia law
- Acquire practical skills to successfully navigate legal frameworks for careers in local government planning or the private sector

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Foundations of law and government structure:** Introduction to key terms; organization of federal, state, and local governments; organization of federal and state court systems; sources of law (common law, constitutional, statutory, regulatory, and local ordinances)
- **The power to control land use locally:** History of land use systems; the police power; roles of governing bodies, planning commissions, boards of zoning appeals, zoning administrators, and planners; legislative, ministerial, and quasi-judicial powers
- **Constitutional and statutory limitations on land use regulation:** Dillon Rule and home rule; federal supremacy and preemption (Telecom Act, RLUIPA, Fair Housing Act, ADA); First, Second, Fourth, Fifth, and Fourteenth Amendment constraints; regulatory takings, eminent domain, and exactions
- **Comprehensive planning:** Elements and contents of the comprehensive plan; procedures to adopt and amend; legal considerations; role of the plan in land use decision-making
- **Zoning ordinances and district types:** Types of zoning ordinances (inclusionary/exclusionary); elements of zoning text and maps; conventional and alternative districts (smart growth, form-based, overlay, PUDs, floating zones, TDR districts); procedures to adopt and amend
- **Zoning procedures and decision-making:** Notice and hearing requirements; staff reports; proffers and impact fees; conditions on special use permits; public hearing conduct; creating a defensible record; frameworks for making decisions on zoning map/text amendments, special use permits, and certificates of appropriateness
- **Administering the zoning ordinance:** Role of the zoning administrator; interpreting and classifying uses; nonconforming uses; vested rights; enforcement; variances; appeals to the board of zoning appeals; moratoria
- **Select contemporary planning and zoning issues:** Affordable housing; environmental protection (air, water, soil); sustainability and renewable energy; flooding and climate risk; agricultural land preservation; historic and urban preservation; transportation and transit-oriented development
- **Subdivision and site plan regulation:** Elements of subdivision and site plan ordinances; plat and site plan review procedures; adequate public facilities requirements; transportation, erosion/sediment control, and stormwater management
- **Judicial review, related laws, and attorney collaboration:** Standing, exhaustion of remedies, and judicial remedies; FOIA and Conflicts of Interest Act; civil liability for zoning officials; working with government or client attorneys; attorney-client privilege

## Advising Notes
UAP 5554 is well suited for MURP students pursuing careers in local government planning, land development consulting, or any practice area requiring fluency in planning law and regulatory frameworks. The course fills a critical role in the MURP curriculum by grounding students in the legal context within which all planning decisions occur, covering constitutional limits, zoning procedures, comprehensive planning, and subdivision regulation with a strong Virginia-law emphasis. No planning-specific prerequisites are listed beyond graduate standing, making it accessible early in a student's program, though students with some exposure to planning practice or policy may find the material more immediately applicable. Offered online-synchronously on Wednesday evenings, this course is especially convenient for Arlington/NCR students, many of whom are working professionals, and the adjunct instructor brings nearly three decades of local government attorney experience — including as Albemarle County Attorney — that provides exceptional real-world depth.
