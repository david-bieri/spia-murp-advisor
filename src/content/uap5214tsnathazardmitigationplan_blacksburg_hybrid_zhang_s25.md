## Course Description
This course introduces students to the concepts and planning tools of disaster resilience and hazard mitigation planning, with a primary focus on fluvial and coastal floods. Topics span the physical processes of flood hazards, hazard mapping, and flood hazard mitigation policies and planning at federal, state, and local levels. The course employs a project-based learning approach centered on an interdisciplinary research project examining how Virginia communities use flood hazard maps and land use policies for flood mitigation.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Describe the concept of disaster resilience, including its conceptualization and measurement
- Describe flood hazards' geological and atmospheric processes, as well as terminologies for flood hazard frequencies and magnitude
- Interpret flood hazard maps and distinguish between flood hazard and flood risk
- Demonstrate familiarity with planning tools for hazard mitigation, especially for flood hazards
- Collaborate effectively with colleagues to define key problems and solution characteristics in flood disaster resilience
- Understand the planning process for hazard mitigation planning and the main components of a local mitigation plan

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Course introduction and problem framing:** Overview of disaster resilience concepts and the semester's interdisciplinary research project
- **Disaster resilience concepts:** Conceptualization, measurement, and stakeholder roles in building community disaster resilience
- **Flood hazard science:** Physical and atmospheric processes of flooding, terminology for frequency and magnitude, and flood hazard modeling
- **Hazard mitigation planning:** Planning process, components of a local hazard mitigation plan, and flood frequency/magnitude estimation exercises
- **Flood risk reduction framework:** Strategies and frameworks for reducing flood risk at community and regional scales
- **National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP):** Federal flood insurance policy, program structure, and implications for local planning
- **FEMA Flood Hazard Maps:** Interpretation, practical use in local planning, and limitations/uncertainties of current mapping tools
- **Floodplain ordinances and land use regulation:** Content analysis of local floodplain ordinances, zoning designations, and residential development regulations in floodplains
- **Flood-related data sources:** Data collection methods, relevant databases, and integration into local flood policy
- **Stakeholder perspectives and knowledge co-production:** Interview-based research with floodplain administrators and engineers on map usability and community flood policymaking

## Advising Notes
UAP 5214 is well-suited for MURP students concentrating in environmental planning, land use, or hazard mitigation, as well as students interested in climate adaptation and community resilience. The course fills a core applied planning niche by integrating federal flood policy (NFIP, FEMA mapping), local land use regulation, and hands-on research with Virginia communities, making it highly relevant to students pursuing careers in local or regional planning practice. No formal prerequisites are listed, but students will benefit from foundational exposure to land use planning and GIS. The course's project-based structure and team assignments involving stakeholder interviews and floodplain ordinance analysis provide strong professional portfolio material for students interested in hazard mitigation planning as a specialization.
