## Course Description
This course introduces a variety of intermediate quantitative research approaches in statistics, emphasizing discrete methods and multivariate analysis. Students are exposed to key methods including their assumptions, techniques of application, and interpretation, with a strong emphasis on applied, hands-on learning using the R programming language. Topics range from OLS regression and its alternatives to spatial analysis, factor analysis, and cluster analysis in the context of urban and regional planning research.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Work with the R programming language for statistical analysis
- Draw inferences informed by statistical theory from samples
- Recognize situations in statistical analysis where one or more assumptions are violated
- Apply non-linear and other intermediate statistical methods to address planning, policy, or engineering questions ill-suited for OLS regression
- Critically assess the concepts, methods, and conclusions of an academic paper using intermediate statistical methods in a planning, policy, or engineering study
- Frame a research question, collect and analyze data using intermediate statistical methods, draw conclusions, and prepare a manuscript suitable for publication in an academic journal

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Module 1 – Introduction to R** (Weeks 1–2): Course orientation, R basics, data wrangling with tidyverse
- **Module 2 – Introduction to Urban Analytics** (Weeks 3–4): Data acquisition, cleaning, and wrangling; data visualization and mapping with ggplot2 and spatial tools
- **Module 3 – Regression Analysis** (Weeks 5–7): Linear/OLS regression review; binomial and multinomial logit regression; Poisson and negative binomial regression for count data
- **Module 4 – Spatial Analysis** (Weeks 8–9): Spatial data analysis and GIS in R; spatial regression and spatial econometrics
- **Module 5 – Advanced Data Techniques** (Weeks 10–11): Factor analysis and principal components analysis; cluster analysis
- **Module 6 – Special Topics** (Weeks 12–13): Time series analysis, multilevel modeling, structural equation modeling; network analysis, instrumental variables, and mixed-methods research
- **Weeks 14–16:** Thanksgiving break (no class); optional work session; final research paper presentations

## Advising Notes
UAP 5494 is well-suited for MURP students who have completed an introductory statistics or quantitative methods course and are ready to advance to intermediate and applied multivariate techniques; students without prior exposure to probability, hypothesis testing, and regression are explicitly advised not to enroll. The course is a strong fit for students pursuing research-oriented careers or planning to write a thesis, as it culminates in a journal-style research paper using methods covered in class. It is offered at both the Arlington/NCR (Ballston) and Blacksburg campuses simultaneously, making it accessible to students at either location. The heavy use of R throughout the course gives students a marketable technical skill, and the applied, hands-on approach — with minimal abstract mathematics — makes advanced methods accessible to planning students from diverse undergraduate backgrounds.
