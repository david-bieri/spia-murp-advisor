## Course Description
This course examines current strategies and examples of planning for parks, greenways, and open space at the local, regional, state, and national levels. It covers the history of park planning, philosophies of park function (including national parks), park typologies, and issues of equity, scale, environmental impacts, and economic benefits. The course also explores open space planning in both rural and suburban contexts.

## Learning Objectives
- Understand the history of park planning in the United States, park typologies, and the multiple functions of parks and open space
- Appreciate the benefits of parks, open space, and greenways and how those benefits are calculated
- Understand methodologies of contemporary park and open space planning
- Analyze issues of park accessibility, use, and values across different ethnic and socioeconomic groups
- Examine the role of greenways in linking parks and enhancing park experience
- Learn about the private land conservation movement and methods for conserving land
- Understand the evolution of the U.S. National Park system and challenges of managing large conservation landscapes
- Consider the value of wilderness and issues of wilderness management
- Analyze the function, management, and performance of individual parks

## Advising Notes
UAP 5424 is well suited for MURP students with interests in environmental planning, land conservation, parks administration, or urban green infrastructure, as well as those pursuing work in public agencies, land trusts, or nonprofit organizations. The course integrates equity analysis, policy frameworks, and planning methodologies, making it a strong complement to environmental and community development concentrations within the MURP curriculum. No prerequisites are stated, but students should be prepared for weekly readings, active discussion participation, and a substantive final paper. The Arlington/NCR campus offering reflects Dr. Mastran's Northern Virginia–based instruction, and the required park field trip adds a hands-on applied learning component distinctive to this course.
