## Course Description
This studio course prepares students with current approaches to international development practice as implemented by leading actors today. Students learn traditional project planning tools used by development organizations as well as alternative processes, covering project development, planning, management, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation. Emphasis is placed on synthesizing and practicing skills through the preparation of a complete proposal for an international development project or program.

## Learning Objectives
Upon completion of the studio, students will be able to develop a complete proposal for an international development project/program, including:
- A problem analysis
- A needs assessment and stakeholder analysis
- A gender analysis and discussion
- A clear set of goals and objectives supported by a logframe analysis
- An assessment of resources and organizational ability
- A proposed work plan and timeframe
- A budget
- A monitoring and evaluation plan
- A plan for the successful implementation of the project

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Studio Introduction & Grant Opportunity Exploration:** Overview of the studio structure; students identify potential USAID or MCC international development project opportunities (Weeks 1–2)
- **The USAID Program Cycle and CLA Framework:** Program cycle policy, project design and implementation processes, and U.S. strategic planning context (Week 2)
- **Project Proposal Fundamentals:** Guest speaker presentation of a real grant proposal; introduction to logframe concepts (Week 3)
- **Development Planning Theory:** Planning in the context of international development, strategic planning, diversifying planning perspectives, and problem analysis (Week 4)
- **Systems Thinking and Problem-Driven Solutions:** Systems practice mindsets and approaches to developing project proposals that respond to well-defined problems (Week 5)
- **Logical Framework Analysis:** Lecture and group activity developing logframes; means-ends analysis, indicators of achievement, and tools for prioritization (Week 6)
- **Gender Analysis in International Development:** Guest speaker on women and gender; gender mainstreaming standards; presentations of emerging project alternatives (Week 7)
- **Project Alternatives Review:** Students present development project concepts to external guest reviewers for critical and constructive feedback (Week 9)
- **Project Budgeting:** Budgeting processes for international development projects, including budget templates, salary scales, per diem rates, and subaward definitions (Week 11)
- **Implementation, Monitoring, and Evaluation:** Approaches to project implementation, M&E frameworks, stakeholder analysis, participatory M&E methods (Week 12)
- **Working Studios and Final Presentations:** Working studio sessions for proposal refinement, practice presentations, and final presentations to a panel of international development experts (Weeks 13–16)

## Advising Notes
UAP 5764 is a capstone-style applied studio course well suited to MURP students with a concentration or strong interest in international development planning, as it requires students to produce a complete, professional-quality international development project proposal by semester's end. The course requires UAP 4764 or equivalent as a prerequisite, making it appropriate for students who have completed foundational international development coursework. The course is offered in a hybrid format with Blacksburg students attending in person and NCR students participating synchronously via Zoom under a separate CRN, so advising staff should ensure students register for the correct section. Distinctive features include guest practitioners from leading development organizations (USAID, ACDI/VOCA, iDE), external proposal review sessions, and a final presentation to a panel of international development experts.
