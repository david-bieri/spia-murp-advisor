## Course Description
This course provides graduate-level concepts central to effective financial management in governmental, nonprofit, and nongovernmental organizations. It prepares students with an understanding and working knowledge of the role of finance in complex organization management, emphasizing principles over technique. Topics include planning and budgeting, cash and debt management, resource acquisition, accounting and financial reporting, auditing, internal controls, and financial condition assessment.

## Learning Objectives
- Become familiar with the fundamental concepts of financial management in public and nonprofit organizations
- Employ financial management tools in analyzing and understanding the financial condition of public and nonprofit organizations
- Become familiar with basic literature (historical and current) in public and nonprofit financial management
- Complete a critical analysis of a substantive financial management concept as applied to a public or nonprofit organization
- Develop the ability to use financial documents for policy analysis, management decision-making, and assessing financial condition

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Module I — Financial Management, Planning, and Budgeting:** Introduction to public financial management; differences among governmental, nonprofit, and for-profit entities; the budget process; budgeting concepts and techniques; capital planning and budgeting; long-term financing and debt management
- **Module II — Implementation and Controlling Results:** Resource acquisition and grant management; partnerships and intergovernmental relations; treasury administration, investments, and pension funds; budget monitoring, performance management, and internal control systems
- **Module III — Reporting Results:** Accounting and auditing standards (GAAP, GASB, FASB, IRS Form 990); financial statement concepts including the Annual Comprehensive Financial Report (ACFR), Statement of Net Position, Statement of Activities, and Cash Flow Statement
- **Module IV — Financial Condition Analysis and Resiliency:** Financial condition analysis frameworks; fiscal sustainability and resiliency; analysis of governmental and nonprofit financial health
- **Module V — Finale:** Student research paper presentations; synthesis and integration of course themes
- **Recurring:** Student-led discussion leadership; cumulative milestone reports building toward the final research paper

## Advising Notes
SPIA 5544 is well-suited for MURP students interested in the financial management dimensions of public sector and nonprofit work, particularly those pursuing careers in local government planning, housing finance, nonprofit management, or community development. The course is a required component of the Graduate Certificate in Public and Nonprofit Financial Management and may also count toward the Graduate Certificate in Nonprofit and Nongovernmental Management, making it a strong option for students pursuing dual credentials. There are no stated prerequisites, but students should be comfortable with graduate-level academic writing and basic quantitative reasoning; the Excel Essentials assignment suggests the course builds foundational data skills. Delivered fully online via synchronous Zoom sessions on Wednesday evenings, this course is accessible to students at any VT campus or location, and the capstone research paper allows students to tailor their analysis to an organization directly relevant to their planning interests.
