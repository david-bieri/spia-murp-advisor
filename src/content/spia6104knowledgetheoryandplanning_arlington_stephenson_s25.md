## Course Description
The philosophy of social science is a meta-theoretical field that investigates how humans make sense of their lived social reality, drawing on political science, sociology, anthropology, economics, human geography, and related disciplines. The course explores enduring philosophical questions about human character, ways of being, and what forms of inquiry are most appropriate for describing and understanding human behavior. Students are not told what to think, but are guided to grapple seriously with theoretical matters and to develop their own informed judgments.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Grasp alternative epistemological and ontological positions in the social scientific literature, including debates about community values, human reasoning, and environmental determinism
- Connect the evolution of philosophical debates concerning the foundations and roles of social science inquiry
- Ascertain the philosophical positions of authors even when those positions are not stated explicitly
- Reflect critically on one's own ontological and epistemological perspectives when designing and conducting research
- Develop independent judgments concerning ongoing controversies in this meta-theoretical field

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Core Concerns and Foundations:** Introduction to the philosophy of social science; basic epistemological and ontological frameworks (weeks 1–2)
- **Social Explanation and Human Behavior:** Naturalism, positivism, and the limits of scientific explanation in social contexts (weeks 3–4)
- **Alternate Perspectives I–III:** Interpretivism, hermeneutics, critical theory, and feminist/poststructuralist approaches (weeks 4–6)
- **Ethics and Interpretation:** Ethical dimensions of social inquiry; challenges of value-laden research (weeks 7–8)
- **Recent Arguments in the Field:** Contemporary developments and debates in philosophy of social science (week 9)
- **Moral Theory:** MacIntyre's *After Virtue* and the role of virtue ethics in understanding social life (weeks 10–11)
- **Critical Realism:** Buch-Hansen and Nielsen; structure, agency, and mechanisms (week 12)
- **Planning Theory:** Beauregard's application of philosophical foundations to planning practice (week 13)
- **Course Wrap-Up and Final Discussion:** Synthesis across semester themes (week 14)

## Advising Notes
SPIA 6104 is a reading- and discussion-intensive doctoral seminar in meta-theory, best suited for MURP students with strong interests in the philosophical and epistemological foundations of planning and policy research, particularly those who plan to pursue academic careers or research-focused professional roles. The course fulfills a theory requirement and pairs especially well with research methods coursework, as it equips students to critically examine the knowledge claims and ontological assumptions underlying planning practice and inquiry. The syllabus explicitly notes that the course may be used in doctoral or master's degree plans across SPIA programs or ASPECT. Students should be prepared for a heavy and variable reading load and should plan their semester calendar accordingly from the outset.
