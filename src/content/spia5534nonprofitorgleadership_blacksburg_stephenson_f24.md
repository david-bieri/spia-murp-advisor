## Course Description
This seminar acquaints students with the foundations and role of leadership and governance of civil society organizations, examining relationships among leadership, management, and governance as imperatives for nonprofit and nongovernmental organizations. It explores multiple conceptions of leadership—including servant, adaptive, transformative, humble, and ethical—as they relate to nonprofit and NGO operating environments. The course also examines accountability structures, board stewardship, and the influence of neoliberal epistemology on organizational governance.

## Learning Objectives
- Describe the role of leadership vis-à-vis the evolving social, political, and economic challenges confronting nonprofit and nongovernmental organizations
- Sketch the accountability environments of nonprofit and nongovernmental organizations and their relationship to effective leadership and governance
- Identify links between nonprofit organization leaders, trustees, stakeholder communities, and effective governance
- Identify major governance challenges—including the influence of neoliberal epistemology—and suggest strategies to address them
- Compare central questions of leadership and governance across nonprofit and nongovernmental organizations
- Articulate an individual orientation or ethos to leadership and provide a rationale for that perspective

## Advising Notes
This seminar is well suited for MURP students with interests in nonprofit management, community development, civil society organizations, or public-private partnerships, as it provides both theoretical grounding and practical frameworks for organizational leadership and governance. It builds explicitly on related SPIA courses such as Nonprofit Organization and Management and NGOs and International Development, making it most valuable when taken after foundational nonprofit coursework. The course is designated for graduate credit and may be applied across SPIA master's and doctoral programs; fourth-year undergraduates may enroll with permission but are held to the same standards. The seminar's emphasis on developing a personal leadership philosophy, conducting organizational governance analysis, and engaging in substantive weekly discussion makes it particularly valuable for students pursuing careers in nonprofit leadership, NGO management, or public administration.
