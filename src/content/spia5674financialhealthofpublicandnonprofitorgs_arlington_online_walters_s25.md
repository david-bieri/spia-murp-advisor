## Course Description
This course examines the concepts and skills needed to effectively assess the financial health of public and nonprofit organizations and foster financial resiliency in support of organizational missions. It provides foundational tools through a review of financial condition analysis approaches and a broad assessment of fiscal environments, including financial factors, socioeconomic conditions, and intergovernmental factors. The course also analyzes management and oversight elements—strategic and long-term financial planning, financial policies, budget practices, and institutional factors—and identifies techniques for diagnosing fiscal stress and building financial resiliency and sustainability.

## Learning Objectives
- Assess the financial condition of public and nonprofit organizations using multiple analytical frameworks
- Analyze socioeconomic, intergovernmental, and institutional factors that affect fiscal environments
- Evaluate financial factors including revenues, expenditures, fund balances, debt, and capital infrastructure
- Examine management and oversight practices—including strategic planning and financial policies—that affect organizational financial health
- Diagnose fiscal stress and develop recommendations for building financial resiliency and sustainability
- Communicate financial analysis findings effectively through written research and professional presentations

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Module I — Introduction to Financial Condition Analysis** (Sessions 1–2, Jan. 27–Feb. 3): Why municipalities matter, state and local fiscal facts, defining financial condition; readings from Finkler et al. and Maher et al.
- **Module II — Socioeconomic Factors and Institutional Framework** (Sessions 2–4, Feb. 3–17): Economic conditions, community needs, disaster risks, intergovernmental and political factors; Excel Essentials Assignment due Feb. 10
- **Module III — Financial Factors** (Sessions 5–9, Feb. 24–Mar. 31): Approaches to financial condition assessment; revenues, expenditures, and operating results; fund balance and liquidity; debt and liabilities; capital maintenance and infrastructure; Milestone #1 due Mar. 3, Group Assignment due Mar. 24; Spring Break Mar. 10
- **Module IV — Management and Oversight** (Sessions 10–11, Apr. 7–14): Strategic and long-term financial planning, financial policies, budgeting practices, accountability and control; Milestone #2 due Apr. 7
- **Module V — Financial Resiliency and Sustainability** (Sessions 12–13, Apr. 21–28): Fiscal stress vs. fiscal distress, diagnosing fiscal distress, strategies for building resiliency and sustainability; Milestone #3 due Apr. 21
- **Module VI — Finale** (Session 14, May 5): Final research paper presentations; Final paper due May 9

## Advising Notes
SPIA 5674 is well suited for MURP students with interests in public finance, municipal governance, community development, or nonprofit management, as it builds direct competency in diagnosing organizational fiscal health—a skill highly applicable to planning practice in both government and nonprofit sectors. The course follows a scaffolded research paper structure (three milestones culminating in a 10–15 page final paper with a presentation), making it a strong fit for students seeking applied writing and analytical development. Delivered entirely online via synchronous Zoom sessions on Monday evenings, it is accessible to students across Virginia Tech's distributed campuses, including working professionals. No formal prerequisites are listed, but students with prior exposure to public budgeting or financial management concepts will be well positioned to engage with the advanced practitioner-oriented readings.
