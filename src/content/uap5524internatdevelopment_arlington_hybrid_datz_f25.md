## Course Description
This course explores the contentious concept of economic "development" as intellectual paradigm, political ideology, geopolitical strategy, and varied paths toward economic growth and poverty alleviation. It examines major theories in the field from the 1930s to the present, with attention to the role of ideas in development policy, foreign aid, debt relief, and China's role in South-South lending and investment. The course takes a transdisciplinary approach drawing on political science, economics, sociology, anthropology, geography, history, and practitioner perspectives.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Critically analyze the concept of "development" across intellectual, ideological, and geopolitical dimensions
- Understand major development theories and their historical evolution from modernization to neoliberalism and beyond
- Evaluate the role of foreign aid, international financial institutions, and domestic institutions in shaping development outcomes
- Assess contemporary debates around sovereign debt relief, restructuring, and China's emergence as a global lender and investor
- Apply transdisciplinary analytical frameworks to understand persistent challenges in middle- and low-income countries

## Course Topics and Schedule

- **Weeks 1–3:** Introduction to international development; Cold War modernization theory (TVA model, Rostow's stages, modernization as ideology)
- **Weeks 4–5:** Foreign aid since the Cold War — allocation, private philanthropy, domestic constraints; dismantling of USAID and DFID/FCDO under Trump and UK conservative governments
- **Week 6:** Domestic institutions and development; *Why Nations Fail*; randomized controlled trials and poverty alleviation (*Poor Economics*)
- **Weeks 7–9:** The developmental state and import substitution industrialization; the neoliberal turn and the Washington Consensus; neoliberalism's record in Latin America and Eastern Europe; resurgent state capitalism
- **Weeks 10–11:** Trade and development — global value chains, South-South trade, tariffs and developing countries; services-led development (India, Vietnam, globotics)
- **Weeks 12–13:** Sovereign debt relief — the HIPC Initiative and its lessons; contemporary debt restructuring and China's role as a creditor
- **Week 15:** China's broader role in global development — Belt and Road Initiative, China-Africa engagement, competitive infrastructure investment, and Western donor responses

## Advising Notes
This course suits MURP students with interests in international planning, global development policy, comparative political economy, or public administration in a global context. It fulfills an elective role within the MURP curriculum, particularly for students concentrating in international development, economic development, or policy analysis. No prerequisites beyond good graduate standing are required, making it accessible to first-semester students. A distinctive feature is the course's strong emphasis on current events — including the dismantling of USAID and evolving China-South dynamics — alongside rigorous theoretical and historical grounding; students should expect substantial reading loads drawn from political science, economics, and policy literatures.
