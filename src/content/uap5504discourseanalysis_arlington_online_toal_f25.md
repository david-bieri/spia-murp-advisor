## Course Description

This course examines the concept of discourse as a central element of contemporary social science, tracing its development across constructivist, Marxist/post-Marxist, Foucauldian, feminist, and critical discourse analysis traditions. It addresses discourse analysis as both socio-political theory and method, with a focus on the nexus of language and power in contemporary social and political structures. The course does not cover linguistic conversation analysis or quantitative approaches, but instead examines how discourse relates to relations of power, dominance, and control.

## Learning Objectives

- Develop familiarity with major traditions of discourse analysis in the social sciences, including framing theory, narrative analysis, Marxist/post-Marxist thought, and Foucauldian analysis
- Understand key theoretical concepts such as ideology, hegemony, interpellation, floating signifiers, power/knowledge, and problematization
- Critically engage with debates over the role of discourse in shaping political, social, and environmental issues including security and climate change
- Apply critical discourse analysis (CDA) as a method to textual and multimodal materials
- Demonstrate mastery of assigned readings and theoretical arguments through structured verbal examination

## Course Topics and Schedule

- **Module 1 — Frames & Narratives:** Introduction to discourse analysis; framing theory (Goffman, social movements, media studies); narrative analysis and national security (exam: September 12)
- **Module 2 — Power, Hegemony & Discourse (Marxist+ Tradition):** Ideology, cultural hegemony (Gramsci), structural Marxism (Althusser), post-Marxism (Laclau and Mouffe), Essex School, populism (exam: October 3)
- **Module 3 — Power, Knowledge, Subjectivity (Foucauldian Discourse Analysis):** Archaeology of discursive practices, genealogy, power/knowledge, problematization; applications to environmental politics and geopolitics (exam: October 24)
- **Module 4 — Security Discourses:** Securitization theory and climate change; ecological security; threat discourses and the construction of security (exam: November 14)
- **Module 5 — Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA):** CDA as theory and method; argumentation analysis; multimodal CDA; application to students' own research interests (exam: December 12)

## Advising Notes

UAP 5504 is a theory- and reading-intensive graduate course best suited for MURP students with strong backgrounds in social science, political theory, or qualitative methods who are comfortable engaging with abstract theoretical frameworks. It is particularly relevant for students interested in qualitative research methodologies, planning theory, environmental politics, or policy discourse, and provides a strong foundation for thesis or dissertation work involving textual or political analysis. The course is cross-listed as GIA/PSCI/UAP 5504 and draws students from multiple graduate programs, which enriches disciplinary exchange; however, Dr. Toal explicitly cautions that beginning master's students without prior exposure to political or social theory may find the content challenging. Delivered fully online and asynchronously, this course is well-suited to Arlington/NCR students, though all exams require availability on Friday afternoons via Zoom.
