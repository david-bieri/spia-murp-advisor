## Course Description
This graduate seminar acquaints students with the evolving role of nongovernmental organizations (NGOs) in international politics and development. The course explores theory concerning civil society and social change generally, and NGOs more particularly, while analyzing the roles NGOs play in development politics and service delivery across multiple analytical scales. It examines interactions among NGOs, states, local jurisdictions, international organizations, and other civil society entities.

## Learning Objectives
- Understand and critically evaluate the strategic and operating environments of international development
- Examine various analytical frameworks investigating and informing the roles and practice of NGOs in international development
- Analyze the diverse relationships among NGOs and other actors involved with international development
- Identify the various factors that mediate the possible actions and outcomes of NGO development initiatives in the contexts in which they operate

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Introduction and the realities of poverty and oppression** — Course framing; *Behind the Beautiful Forevers* as a ground-level portrait of development challenges (Jan. 16–23)
- **Social dynamics, social change, and development** — Macro-level frameworks for understanding development (*Woolcock*; Jan. 30)
- **Perspectives on NGOs and their roles in development** — Organizational roles, typologies, and relationships with states and communities (*Lewis et al.; Davies*; Feb. 6–27)
- **Considering culture and social development** — Cultural dimensions of development theory and practice (*Clammer*; Mar. 12–19)
- **NGOs and "the project" in development** — Critical examination of project-based development models and their limitations (*Scott*; Mar. 26–Apr. 2)
- **Testing "project" thinking through evidence and experience** — Negotiating knowledge, evidence, and practitioner experience in development (*Hayman et al.*; Apr. 9–16)
- **What "works" in development?** — Synthesizing lessons from cases and theory about effective development practice (*Tiwari*; Apr. 23–30)

## Advising Notes
This course is well-suited for MURP students with interests in international development, civil society, global governance, or nonprofit and NGO management. It offers a theoretically grounded but practice-oriented examination of how NGOs operate within broader development politics, making it relevant for students pursuing careers in international organizations, INGOs, or development consulting. The course is based in Blacksburg with a hybrid participation option, so Arlington/NCR-based students should confirm with the instructor whether remote attendance is a viable long-term arrangement. The intensive reading load (eight full texts) and seminar format require strong self-direction; students should plan their semester schedule carefully given staggered deadlines for three analytic essays, a research paper, and a reflective essay.
