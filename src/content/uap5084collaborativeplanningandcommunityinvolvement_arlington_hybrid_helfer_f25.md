## Course Description
A core aspect of urban planning and public policy-making is the practice of engaging people to enhance policies, programs, or services at the community level and to respond to unmet public needs. This course focuses on the vital role planners and policy-makers play in facilitating community involvement, guiding students in identifying and selecting models and strategies for engagement, and developing their toolkit for involving and mobilizing stakeholders. The course integrates theory, practical exercises, and real-world applications to develop students' effectiveness as community involvement professionals.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Critique models of deliberative democracy, framing their key features in relation to stakeholder engagement and deliberative rationality.
- Analyze tools and approaches to collaborative planning, community involvement, and alternative dispute resolution in the public sector, including their associated challenges and opportunities.
- Appraise overarching challenges in collaborative planning processes and critique strategies for addressing them.
- Analyze the roles stakeholders can and should play in deliberating, planning, and decision-making, and their rights to information.
- Display skills relevant to the practice of facilitation, mediation, and negotiation.
- Select appropriate tools and approaches for collaborative planning and community involvement across different planning and policy contexts.
