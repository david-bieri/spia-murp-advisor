## Course Description
The Economic Development Studio is a hands-on, professional experience designed for graduate students nearing degree completion who have an interest in economic development. Students function as planning staff or consultants, taking ownership of a real client deliverable — in Spring 2025, an assessment of risks and resilience strategies for outdoor recreation economies in the New River Valley region, conducted on behalf of the New River Regional Commission. The course requires substantial work outside of class and a strong commitment to team-based, professional-quality output.

## Learning Objectives
- Conduct a real-world study involving data gathering, analysis, writing, and presentation.
- Become familiar with tools and strategies in economic development.
- Understand project management skills and requirements.
- Gain experience working in a team for an external client.
- Develop professional judgment in scoping, researching, and communicating planning work products.

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Weeks 1–2:** Introduction to economic development, asset-based development, and the outdoor economy; impacts of recent disasters (e.g., Hurricane Helene) on recreation-dependent communities
- **Week 3:** Research design and scoping; qualitative methods, interviewing, and survey design
- **Weeks 4–5:** Project scoping, workplan development, and initial stakeholder engagement; regional context for the New River Valley
- **Week 6:** Regional overview and stakeholder mapping; preliminary literature review and data collection tool development
- **Weeks 7 & 9:** Progress reports and team work sessions; primary data collection and analysis
- **Week 10:** Presentation of preliminary findings; interim report outline
- **Weeks 11–13:** Dress rehearsal, continued drafting, and preparation for final deliverables
- **Weeks 14–15:** Draft report review; final presentation to client (New River Regional Commission)
- **Week 16:** Final report submission

## Advising Notes
UAP 5774 is a capstone-style studio course best suited for MURP students in the later stages of their program who have completed foundational coursework in planning methods, economic development, or community development. The course provides rare real-client, real-deliverable experience — working with the New River Regional Commission on outdoor economy resilience — making it especially valuable for students pursuing careers in economic development, rural planning, or community resilience. Students should expect a significant time commitment well beyond the weekly class meeting, as the course is explicitly modeled on professional consulting practice. The course is offered at the Blacksburg campus (1900 Kraft Drive) with a Zoom option; students considering remote participation should confirm expectations directly with Prof. Provo.
