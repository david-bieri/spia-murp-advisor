## Course Description
This studio ("application") course builds on knowledge and skills gained in the MURP core courses by applying them to a real problem or project. Students gain knowledge of planning law and legislation, planning processes and participants, and a planner's roles and responsibilities in plan development and implementation. The course emphasizes practical, professionally relevant skills including writing, public speaking, graphics, data collection and analysis, and project management.

## Learning Objectives
- Develop knowledge of planning law and legislation, planning processes and participants, and planner roles and responsibilities
- Build professional writing skills: plans, staff reports, technical reports, memos, meeting minutes, rules and regulations, and policy statements
- Develop public speaking and presentation skills, including formal presentations and meeting facilitation
- Build graphics skills for reports and presentations, including hand-drawn, computer-generated, data visualizations, and maps
- Strengthen data collection and analysis skills using field surveys, interviews, questionnaires, administrative records, and secondary data
- Develop project and workload management skills, including use of PERT and GANTT charts and balancing competing project demands

## Required Texts and Materials
No required texts. Readings will be drawn from APA website resources and university library materials as needed. The following are suggested optional references:

Students are also expected to independently locate, read, and report on resources relevant to studio projects. Course materials are distributed via a shared Google Drive folder (Canvas is not used).

## Advising Notes
UAP 5125 is the MURP capstone studio course and is designed for students who have completed the core MURP curriculum; it is intended to synthesize and apply prior coursework to real-world planning challenges for a community client. This Blacksburg section, taught by Dr. Zahm, emphasizes hands-on skill development in writing, presentation, data analysis, graphics, and project management — competencies explicitly identified by planning directors as critical for professional success. Students should be aware that Canvas is not used; all materials are shared via Google Drive, and the course schedule and due dates are set collaboratively during the semester. Work completed in the fall may carry forward toward the spring (UAP 5126) grade, so students should plan for continuity across both semesters.
