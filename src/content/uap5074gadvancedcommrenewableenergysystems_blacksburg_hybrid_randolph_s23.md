## Course Description
This course explores the current global energy situation and the emerging transition to clean, sustainable energy, with emphasis on efficiency of use and renewable sources including wind and solar. It covers the science and technology of energy systems in buildings, electricity, and transportation alongside the economic, market, policy, and social dimensions of energy transformation. The 2020 Virginia Tech Climate Action Commitment is used as a real-world case study throughout the course.

## Learning Objectives
- Understand the energy situation and relevant technological, economic, environmental, policy, and geopolitical issues driving the transition to clean energy
- Comprehend the technical nature of energy systems and apply fundamental design concepts for cost-effective efficient and renewable systems at community and site scales
- Apply basic tools for energy and economic analysis involved in energy system design, planning, evaluation, and policy analysis

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Energy Patterns and Fundamentals:** Global energy situation, consequences, future scenarios, energy science basics, and life-cycle analysis
- **Green Buildings and Solar Energy Fundamentals:** Building thermal energy, heat loss, HVAC, retrofits, solar geometry, insolation, and solar thermal collection
- **Whole Building Energy and Review:** Integrated green building design; Midterm exam
- **Sustainable Electricity:** Centralized and decentralized generation, district CHP, smart grid, photovoltaics, and net-zero-energy buildings
- **Wind and Large-Scale Solar:** Wind energy systems and utility-scale solar deployment
- **Transportation Energy:** Vehicle efficiency, electric vehicles, alternative fuels, biomass, shared and sustainable mobility
- **Energy Planning and Policy:** Government market transformation policies, international and federal climate and energy policy, state utility policy, and community energy/climate action planning
- **Student Project Presentations and Optional Final Exam**

## Advising Notes
This course is well suited to MURP students with interests in energy planning, climate action, environmental policy, and sustainable infrastructure, and it aligns closely with concentration areas in environmental planning and land use policy. The course provides both quantitative analytical skills (energy calculations, economic analysis) and qualitative policy literacy, making it valuable preparation for students pursuing energy-focused planning careers or research. The hybrid format — with synchronous Zoom sessions and asynchronous recorded lectures — offers scheduling flexibility, though students should be prepared for a quantitatively demanding workload including six assignments and a term project. No formal prerequisites are listed, but students should be comfortable with applied analytical reasoning; the course cross-lists as UAP 4394 (undergraduate) and UAP 5074 (graduate), with graduate students expected to meet the higher-level 5074 expectations.
