## Course Description
UAP 5584 provides an introduction to the key ideas, actors, and institutions that influence the development of environmental politics and policy in the US and globally. The course mixes insights from the physical and social sciences with the professional requirements of public policy development, examining policy developments that move beyond the conceptual divisions of the 1970s to reflect the increasingly interwoven nature of social and environmental issues since the 2000s. Students develop conceptual/theoretical and operational/practical skills necessary for interpreting and communicating ecologically important policy innovations, with a strong service-learning component and emphasis on realist epistemology.

## Learning Objectives
- Develop conceptual and theoretical skills for interpreting environmental politics and policy in the US and globally
- Develop operational and practical skills for communicating and advocating ecologically important policy innovations
- Apply policy analysis frameworks (e.g., Bardach and Patashnik's Eightfold Path) to real-world environmental problems
- Understand the political dimensions of environmental issues, including the roles of denial, interest groups, and institutional actors
- Produce professional-grade applied writing, including policy briefs directed at responsible institutions and office bearers
- Demonstrate ability to condense, present, and defend research in lecture, seminar, or outreach formats

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Policy Analysis Foundations:** Bardach and Patashnik's Eightfold Path; practical frameworks for policy problem-solving (Weeks 2–3)
- **US Environmental Politics and Policy:** Historical development, key actors, institutions, and regulatory frameworks; Rosenbaum's overview of US environmental policy (Weeks 4–5)
- **Energy Policy in the US States:** Clean energy and climate policy battles, interest groups, and the American political economy (Weeks 6–7)
- **The Pivotal Decade — The 1970s:** The Powell Memo, the Trilateral Commission, early neoliberal governance, and the roots of contemporary environmental politics (Week 8)
- **Liberal Authoritarianism and Governance:** Chamayou's genealogy of ungovernable society and its implications for environmental regulation (Weeks 9–10)
- **A Brief History of Denial:** Industrial-scale climate and environmental denial; the role of fossil fuel interests in shaping public discourse and policy (Week 11)
- **Case Studies in Denial:** The climate change counter-movement; critical materials and geopolitics of the energy transition (Weeks 12–13)
- **Applied Presentations and Final Submissions:** Student policy brief presentations; final policy brief and research paper due (Week 15)

## Advising Notes
This course is well suited to MURP students with interests in environmental planning, climate policy, energy governance, or the political dimensions of sustainability and land use. It integrates policy analysis methods with political theory and applied writing skills, making it a strong complement to core MURP planning methods and policy coursework. The course requires graduate standing and carries a substantial writing load — including a 20–30 page policy brief and a 20–25 page research paper — so students should plan their semester accordingly. Offered at the Blacksburg campus by Dr. Scerri, the course's service-learning orientation and emphasis on professional communication (policy briefs, presentations, outreach) provides directly transferable skills for careers in government, NGOs, and environmental consulting.
