## Course Overview

Overview of key planning theories, planning history, and planning processes and
approaches. Equips students with conceptual tools to improve the quality of
deliberation and a critical perspective on contemporary urban planning challenges.

## Quasi-Weekly Reflections (30%)

- Length: approximately 500 words each
- Due: Mondays at noon (before class)
- Visibility: posted in an open discussion forum — visible to all classmates
- Peers can comment on your reflections
- No reflection due on weeks when major assignments are due

Community of practice: Prof. Cowell encourages formation of small reading groups
(3–4 students) to discuss readings before writing. Students may draw on these
discussions in their reflections, but every student must write independently.

Reflection prompts (choose one or more):
- Identify an argument in the readings — what are its strengths and weaknesses?
- Identify something that challenged your prior understanding or worldview
- Identify a theoretical concept and its implications for practice
- Compare and contrast two theoretical concepts from the readings
- Identify a problem in the readings — who is affected, what causes it, how addressed?
- Pick a striking statement and argue whether you agree or disagree

## Group-Based Final Project (25%)

Case study analysis of any city's approach to addressing an urban planning issue.
(e.g., affordable housing, gentrification, environmental injustice, resilience,
climate change, sustainability, transportation)

Requirements:
- Groups of 3–5 students
- 7-page single-spaced executive summary
- Bibliography: at least 20 references (not included in the 7 pages),
  at least 50% peer-reviewed scholarly articles or books
- Presentation (creative format encouraged — video, Prezi, multimedia)
- Topic approval required before beginning

Key deadlines:
- Final project topic and group: February 5
- Final project presentations: April 29
- Final reports due: May 6

## Late Policy

Work submitted late will not be accepted without a note from the Dean of Students
office. Without a Dean of Students excuse, late assignments are marked down by
one full grade for every day late.

Example: An assignment that would have scored B+ becomes:
- B if submitted within the first 24 hours after the due date
- B- if submitted 24–48 hours after the due date
- C+ if submitted 48–72 hours after the due date
(and so on)

7. Late policy: grade reduction per day (Arlington) vs zero credit (Blacksburg)

9. AI policy: none in Spring 2024 syllabus — clarify with Prof. Cowell (Arlington) vs detailed policy (Blacksburg)
10. Office hours: by appointment only (Arlington) vs fixed Monday/Wednesday schedule (Blacksburg)
11. Class format: hybrid/flipped, Monday evenings (Arlington) vs in-person M/W daytime (Blacksburg)
12. Project weight: 25% (Arlington) vs 30% (Blacksburg)
