## Course Description
This course introduces students to the profession of planning and the roles and responsibilities of planners in the public, private, and non-profit sectors. Students learn about the relationships among federal, state, and local agencies in addressing complex "wicked" problems and how those problems affect a variety of stakeholders. The course challenges students to communicate using written, oral, visual, and digital strategies while examining the social, physical, environmental, and economic dimensions of planning.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Understand the roles and responsibilities of planners across public, private, and non-profit sectors
- Analyze complex, multi-stakeholder planning problems through social, physical, environmental, and economic lenses
- Apply professional ethics standards using the AICP Code of Ethics
- Develop and articulate a career strategic plan based on knowledge, skills, and abilities (KSAs) required in the planning profession
- Communicate effectively in written, oral, visual, and digital formats at a professional standard
- Complete foundational program requirements including the Plan of Study (POS) and CITI human subjects research training

## Key Policies

**Attendance:**
Students are expected to attend every class session, arrive on time, remain for the entire session, and come fully prepared (having completed all assigned readings). Participation is informally graded each class session on an A–F scale. Any obvious distraction from computer or phone use results in no attendance credit for that day.

**Late Work:**
Assignments are expected on the stated due dates without exception. The instructor frames deadlines in professional terms: submitting late, incomplete, or sloppy work is analogous to failing to deliver a report to a planning commission and will negatively affect the student's grade. Draft submissions are required before finals and are also assessed.

**Academic Integrity:**
All submitted work is assumed to conform to the Graduate Honor Code and standard academic integrity expectations. Plagiarism and cheating will be reported to the Graduate Honor System. A plea of ignorance is not an acceptable defense; students are responsible for understanding what constitutes a violation.

## Advising Notes
Gateway to Planning (UAP 5014) is the foundational gateway course for all incoming MURP students and should be taken in the first semester of the program; it is offered at both the Blacksburg and Arlington/NCR campuses, with joint sessions connecting both cohorts during the semester. The Blacksburg section, taught by Dr. Zahm, is held in Architecture Annex 111 and carries a strong professional orientation, holding students to workplace-level expectations around deadlines, communication quality, and above-and-beyond effort. The course fulfills several required program milestones simultaneously, including the CITI human subjects training certificate, a draft Plan of Study, and an introduction to the AICP Code of Ethics — making it essential for timely degree progress. It is particularly well-suited to students who benefit from structured career reflection early in their graduate studies and want to connect academic coursework to professional planning practice from day one.
