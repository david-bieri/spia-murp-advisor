## Course Description
This course focuses on the vital role planners and policy-makers play in facilitating community involvement — the engagement of people to enhance policies, programs, or services at the community level or to respond to unmet public needs. Students will identify and select models and strategies for engagement and further develop their professional toolkit for involving and mobilizing stakeholders. The course integrates theory, practical exercises, and real-world applications to challenge how students think about and practice engagement.

## Learning Objectives
- Critique models of deliberative democracy, framing their key features relative to stakeholder engagement and deliberative rationality
- Analyze how various tools and approaches to collaborative planning, community involvement, and alternative dispute resolution can be implemented, including associated challenges and opportunities
- Appraise overarching challenges facing collaborative planning processes and evaluate how they can be ameliorated
- Analyze the roles various stakeholders can and should play in deliberating, planning, and decision-making, and their rights to information
- Display skills relevant to the practice of facilitation, mediation, and negotiation
- Select appropriate tools and approaches for collaborative planning and community involvement across different planning and policy-making contexts

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Course Overview and the Case for Community Engagement** — why engagement matters, introduction to key frameworks (Week 1)
- **Public Participation in the 21st Century** — Arnstein's Ladder, reframing participation strategies (Week 2)
- **Models of Community Engagement** — varieties of participation, citizen participation models and methods, IAP2 Spectrum (Week 3)
- **Deliberative Dialogue Design** — deliberative democracy theory, DIAD model, wicked problems, outreach and analysis (Week 4)
- **Tools and Approaches to Public Participation** — participation scenarios, infrastructure, accessible engagement, listening skills (Week 5)
- **Engagement Toolkit Presentations** — student lightning talks on tools such as deliberative polling, participatory budgeting, design charrettes, citizens' assemblies, and others (Week 6)
- **Situation and Stakeholder Assessment** — conflict assessment, consensus building approaches (Week 7)
- **Negotiation** — BATNA, 3-D negotiation, interest-based approaches (Week 8)
- **Facilitation and Mediation** — facilitation skill development, mediation process, facilitator's toolkit (Week 10)
- **Diversity, Social Justice, and Empowerment** — difference as democratic resource, intercultural planning, equity in engagement (Week 11)
- **Student-Led Case Discussions and Final Presentations** — applied case analyses and group engagement strategy presentations (Weeks 9, 11–12, 15)

## Advising Notes
This course is well suited for MURP students interested in public engagement, deliberative democracy, conflict resolution, and the interpersonal and process dimensions of planning practice. It functions as a core professional skills course in the MURP curriculum, building practical competencies in facilitation, negotiation, stakeholder analysis, and community involvement strategy design. The course is offered in a hybrid format and enrolls students from both the Arlington/NCR and Blacksburg campuses, making it accessible to students at either location, though Arlington students benefit from in-person class sessions. No prerequisites are listed, but the course pairs well with policy analysis, planning theory, and studio courses, as the final group project requires students to develop a real-world engagement strategy that integrates skills built across the semester.
