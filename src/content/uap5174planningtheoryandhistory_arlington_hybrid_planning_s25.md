## Course Description
This course provides an overview of key planning theories, planning history, and planning processes and approaches. It equips students with conceptual tools to improve the quality of deliberation and to develop a critical perspective on the challenges and issues facing contemporary urban planning. Topics are explored through readings, lectures, class discussions, debates, assignments, research projects, and writing exercises.

---

## Learning Objectives
- Compare and contrast different planning philosophies and theories (e.g., rational/comprehensive, incremental, communicative, advocacy)
- Articulate and critique various planning theories
- Evaluate the applicability of different planning theories to planning practice and contemporary planning questions
- Analyze important events, themes, and debates in the history and evolution of planning and examine their influence on current planning thought and practice
- Examine the different ways ethical dilemmas can be framed and addressed in specific cases
- Communicate information effectively and efficiently via memos, oral presentations, and other formats

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Urban Culture and Society:** Classic sociological perspectives on urban life (Simmel, Wirth, Mumford) and contemporary issues of race and space
- **Planning History and Visions:** Historical urban utopias and planning visions (Howard, Le Corbusier, Wright, Olmsted); racial inequality in U.S. planning history
- **Foundations of 20th Century Planning Theory:** Authoritarian modernism, Jane Jacobs, capitalist city planning, zoning, and historical planning currents
- **Goals, Justifications, and Critiques of Planning (Parts I & II):** Rational/incremental models, neoliberalism, sustainable development, resilience, spatial justice, and cosmopolitan urbanism
- **Collaborative and Participatory Planning Theories:** Advocacy and pluralism, communicative planning, citizen participation, community land trusts, and community development
- **Planning Ethics:** Professional ethics, AICP Code of Ethics, socially responsible practice, and the Graduate Honor Code
- **Multicultural Planning Histories and Theories:** Indigenous planning, insurgent planning, Global South urbanism, and social diversity in metropolitan development
- **Debates on Critical Planning Issues:** Compact city/smart growth, gentrification, the American Dream, car-free cities, the Smart City, and urbanism post-COVID
- **Group Final Project Presentations:** Case study analyses of urban planning approaches to contemporary issues in national or international cities

## Advising Notes
UAP 5174 is a foundational required course in the MURP program that introduces students to the intellectual history, core theories, and ethical frameworks of urban and regional planning; it is well-suited as an early-sequence course for incoming MURP students regardless of concentration. The course is offered in a hybrid synchronous/asynchronous format with Monday evening Zoom sessions, making it highly accessible to working professionals in the Arlington/NCR area. Its strong emphasis on equity, diversity, and inclusion (DEI) threads throughout the curriculum, alongside international comparative perspectives, reflecting Virginia Tech's SPIA commitment to socially responsible planning practice. The substantial group final project, structured debates, and weekly reflective writing develop both analytical and professional communication skills central to MURP competencies.
