## Course Description
This course introduces students to computational techniques used in urban planning practice and research, with a focus on Geographic Information Systems (GIS). Topics include fundamental GIS concepts and tools, spatial data structures, relational databases, spatial analysis, and data visualization, all applied to urban planning problems. Using a project-based, hands-on approach, students work with real datasets from the U.S. Census, ACS, USGS, FEMA, and Virginia state agencies to analyze demographic, economic, land use, and environmental issues.

## Learning Objectives
- Describe, distinguish, and apply fundamental GIS concepts, principles, and tools to understand urban social, environmental, and economic issues
- Demonstrate mastery of basic skills and techniques using GIS software
- Identify, diagnose, and offer solutions to urban social, environmental, or economic issues using GIS technology
- Develop and present concise, effective presentations of GIS-based analysis
- Work effectively with students from diverse backgrounds in completing a team project

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Weeks 1–2:** Course overview, introduction to GIS, examples of GIS applications in urban planning
- **Weeks 3–4:** GIS data structures (including Assignment 1 short presentations)
- **Weeks 5–7:** Map design, cartographic principles, map projections, and coordinate systems
- **Week 8:** Working with Census/ACS data in GIS; demographic analysis; midterm reflection
- **Week 9:** Land use analysis and modeling; spatial patterns and spatial reasoning
- **Weeks 10–11:** Geoprocessing for environmental analysis; flood hazard risk assessment and mapping
- **Week 12:** Public participatory GIS; GIS survey tools; ArcGIS Online
- **Weeks 13–16:** Student project workshops and Assignment 5–6 presentations (Week 14: Thanksgiving break — no class)

*Six topical assignments are distributed and presented throughout the semester, each covering a different urban planning application area.*

## Advising Notes
UAP 5114 is a foundational GIS course well suited for MURP students who lack prior GIS experience, as no prerequisites are required. It builds core technical competency in spatial analysis, data management, and visualization that supports a wide range of MURP concentrations, including land use planning, environmental planning, and community development. The course runs as a hybrid section, with Blacksburg students attending in-person and Arlington/NCR students participating via Zoom, making it accessible to students at both campuses; however, Arlington students should confirm computer lab availability at VTRC before relying solely on lab access. The project-based structure using real planning datasets (Census, ACS, FEMA, USGS) makes this course particularly valuable early in a student's program as a foundation for methods-intensive coursework and applied thesis or practicum work.
