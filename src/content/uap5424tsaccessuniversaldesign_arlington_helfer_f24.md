## Course Description
"Planning for ALL—Exploring Accessibility and Universal Design" is an intensive two-day workshop that examines accessibility law, universal design principles, and their application to urban planning practice. Students engage with guest practitioners and community members with lived experience of disability, conduct a field-based accessibility assessment, and reflect on how to incorporate inclusive design into future planning work. The workshop is offered through the Virginia Tech School of Urban Planning in Fall 2024.

## Learning Objectives
- Understand the legal framework governing accessibility in the built environment, including the ADA, ABA, and PROWAG
- Analyze accessibility guidelines (ADAAG, PROWAG) as applied to pedestrian infrastructure, parks, public buildings, and transportation facilities
- Evaluate the relationship between physical environments and functional ability across a range of disability types
- Conduct a field-based accessibility assessment, identifying existing features, barriers, and alternative solutions
- Apply principles of Universal Design to urban planning practice
- Articulate personal commitments and strategies for advancing accessibility and inclusion in planning careers

## Course Topics and Schedule

- **ADA Legal Framework:** Overview of Title II and Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act; ADA, ABA, and PROWAG accessibility guidelines
- **Practitioner Perspectives:** Panel dinner with Arlington County and DC DOT planners discussing real-world accessibility challenges and tools
- **Accessibility Guidelines in Practice:** Small-group exercises applying ADAAG and PROWAG scoping to pedestrian paths, parks, public buildings, and transit facilities
- **Principles of Universal Design:** Introduction to the seven principles of UD and their relevance to urban planning
- **Lived Experience:** Panel with community members with disabilities sharing how the built environment affects navigation and mobility
- **Environment–Function Interface:** Structured analysis of how various disability types (mobility, vision, hearing, cognitive, speech, sensory) interact with environmental features and barriers
- **Community Field Assessment:** Small-group field exercise documenting existing accessibility features, barriers, and alternative solutions in the surrounding urban environment
- **Applying Lessons to Planning Practice:** Synthesis discussion on takeaways, future planning applications, challenges to advancing accessibility, and individual commitments to action
