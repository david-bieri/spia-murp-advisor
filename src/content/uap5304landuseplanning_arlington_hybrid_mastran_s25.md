## Course Description
This course covers the basic concepts, principles, strategies, and tools of local-level urban land use planning, with a focus on real-world planning processes and implementation. Topics include information requirements for planning, policy considerations, environmental analysis, residential and non-residential land use needs, planned communities, and a range of planning tools. The course includes a field trip.

## Learning Objectives
- Discuss historical trends in development and their relationship to contemporary development patterns, plans, and policies
- Explain the relationships among the comprehensive plan and implementation tools such as zoning ordinances, subdivision ordinances, design guidelines, and performance standards
- State the role and significance of state enabling legislation in local planning
- Identify the basic components of a land use plan and evaluate the quality of a comprehensive plan
- Recognize the variety of individuals and organizations involved in planning and development, each with distinct priorities and objectives
- Use basic land use planning vocabulary
- Learn various ways to engage the public in the planning process (community meetings, surveys, pop-up engagements, social media, etc.)
- Develop materials for multiple audiences that are factually accurate, legally consistent, and grammatically correct
- Demonstrate appropriate group process skills including collaboration, organization, productivity, and effectiveness

## Advising Notes
UAP 5304 is a core land use planning course well-suited to MURP students seeking foundational knowledge in comprehensive planning, zoning, and plan implementation, particularly those interested in local government, community development, or environmental planning. The course is offered at the Arlington/NCR campus and follows a hybrid format combining in-person sessions with Zoom and asynchronous components, making it accessible to working professionals in the Northern Virginia region. It includes applied elements such as student-led presentations, a field trip, and a final paper, giving students practical experience communicating planning concepts to diverse audiences. Students should be aware that no explicit prerequisites are listed, but the course's coverage of Virginia planning law and local implementation tools makes it especially valuable early in the MURP program.
