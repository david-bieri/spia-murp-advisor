## Course Description
This course surveys the field of local economic development (ED) planning, beginning with an overview of ED history and its theoretical and methodological foundations. The second segment examines a range of ED programs and policies organized around three themes: industry-targeted programs, place-based development, and people-centered approaches. Case presentations by students and guest speakers provide a practice-oriented context for the material.

## Learning Objectives
- Demonstrate knowledge of the methods and processes of economic development planning.
- Become familiar with the practice of economic development through readings, case studies, and discussion.
- Gain an understanding of economic development programs and policies.
- Distinguish between people-, place-, and industry-based economic development programs and policies.

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Introduction and context** (Weeks 1–3): Course overview; what is economic development?; evaluation and the politics of ED; theoretical and methodological foundations.
- **People-based ED — workforce and jobs** (Weeks 4–6): Job-centered economic development; workforce development programs; debate exercise on ED policy tradeoffs.
- **People-based ED — entrepreneurship** (Weeks 6–8): Entrepreneurship and small business development; racial and spatial dimensions of small business support programs.
- **Place-based ED — large-scale interventions** (Weeks 8–10): Stadiums, prisons, convention centers, and mega-events; guest speakers (TBD).
- **Place-based ED — redevelopment** (Weeks 10–11): Central city revitalization; brownfield redevelopment; older industrial cities.
- **Industry-based ED — incentives and retention** (Weeks 11–13): Business location incentives; industrial retention and business development; CBA case study presentations.
- **The future of economic development** (Weeks 14–15): Industrial retention wrap-up; emerging trends and the future of ED; GOVA regional profile presentations.
- **Integrative thread** (throughout): Progressive reading of *Janesville: An American Story* connecting real-world community decline to course concepts; book review as culminating assignment.
