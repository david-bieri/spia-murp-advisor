## Course Description
This studio examines the intersection of urban inequalities and climate justice, with a focus on how climate change disproportionately burdens historically marginalized communities. Students work as a project team to analyze climate action plans and urban policies across large and megacities in the USA, exploring how historical and current inequalities in housing, water, sanitation, transportation, and public health relate to climate vulnerability and adaptation. The course works toward producing a collaborative final report grounded in climate justice frameworks.

## Learning Objectives
- Understand the conceptual and empirical links between urban inequalities and climate justice
- Analyze climate action plans and related urban policies using qualitative and content analysis methods
- Apply intersectionality frameworks to assess how race, class, and other social dimensions shape climate vulnerability
- Identify inequalities across urban service domains (housing, water, sanitation, transportation, public health, urban heat, flood risk) in specific U.S. cities
- Develop policy recommendations that advance climate justice at the city level
- Collaborate as a planning studio team to produce a professional-quality research report

## Course Topics and Schedule
- **Part I — Climate Action, Climate Justice, and Urban Inequalities:** Introductions, why climate justice matters, urban inequalities and intersectionality frameworks (Weeks 1–5)
- **Content Analysis Methods:** Reviewing and coding climate action plans and related housing, water, public health, and other urban policies (Week 5)
- **Part II — Learning from Inequalities (Housing):** Guest lecture on housing inequality; short note on housing inequality in assigned city (Week 7)
- **Part II — Water, Sanitation, and Public Health:** Guest lectures; short notes on water/sanitation and public health inequalities (Weeks 9–10)
- **Part II — Transportation and Urban Heat:** Guest lectures on transportation inequalities and urban heat; short notes on each topic (Weeks 10–11)
- **Part II — Flood Risk and Climate Mitigation:** Guest lecture; revisiting content analysis in light of access inequalities; short note on climate impacts (Week 12)
- **Part III — Converting Insights into Policy Recommendations:** Guest lectures from practicing environmental and climate planners; identifying city-level policy recommendations; drafting city-level chapters (Weeks 13–14)
- **Final Presentations and Report:** City-level presentations, consolidation of cross-city insights, and submission of final team report (Weeks 14–16)
